## 3. 필드별 연습 — 8단락

### Part A — 수학 (M1~M2)

### M1.
A function f is said to be continuous at a point a if the limit of f(x) as x approaches a is equal to f(a). Intuitively, a continuous function can be drawn without lifting the pencil from the paper. If a function is continuous at every point of an interval, we call it continuous on that interval. Many theorems of calculus, such as the Intermediate Value Theorem and the Extreme Value Theorem, require the function to be continuous. These theorems guarantee the existence of solutions and extrema that would be difficult to prove otherwise.

### M2.
The derivative of a function measures the rate at which the function changes as its input changes. For a product of two functions, the derivative is given by the product rule, which states that the derivative of fg is f'g plus fg'. The quotient rule gives a similar formula for the derivative of a quotient of two functions. Both rules can be derived from the definition of the derivative as a limit, although the algebra is often tedious.

---

### Part B — 물리 (P1~P2)

### P1.
The magnitude of the gravitational force between two objects is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. This law of universal gravitation, formulated by Newton, explains the motion of the planets and the fall of objects on Earth. The gravitational force is always attractive, and it acts along the line joining the centers of the two objects. Although gravity is the weakest of the four fundamental forces, it dominates at astronomical scales.

### P2.
The total momentum of a closed system is conserved when no external forces act on the system. This principle of conservation of momentum follows directly from Newton's third law, which states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction. In a collision between two objects, the momentum lost by one object is gained by the other. Even when the details of the collision forces are unknown, the conservation law allows us to predict the final velocities of the objects.

---

### Part C — 화학 (C1~C2)

### C1.
The equilibrium position of a chemical reaction shifts when the concentration of one of the reactants or products is changed. This behavior is summarized by Le Chatelier's principle, which states that a system at equilibrium responds to a disturbance by shifting in the direction that reduces the disturbance. For example, increasing the concentration of a reactant drives the reaction toward the products. The same principle applies to changes in temperature and pressure.

### C2.
An acid is defined as a substance that donates a proton to another substance in a chemical reaction. A base is defined as a substance that accepts a proton. According to this definition, known as the Brønsted–Lowry theory, acids and bases always come in pairs. The strength of an acid is measured by how readily it donates its proton, which is reflected in the acid dissociation constant Ka.

---

### Part D — 생물 (B1~B2)

### B1.
The structure of a protein determines its function, and a change in the amino acid sequence can alter the shape of the protein. Proteins fold into specific three-dimensional shapes, which are stabilized by various types of bonds and interactions between the amino acids. If the folding is disrupted, for example by high temperature or extreme pH, the protein loses its function. This process is called denaturation.

### B2.
The movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration is called osmosis. The membrane allows water to pass through but blocks the movement of many solutes. When a cell is placed in a solution with a lower concentration of solutes than its interior, water enters the cell and it swells. Conversely, in a solution with a higher concentration of solutes, water leaves the cell and it shrinks.
