# Physics Curriculum v1: SAT Regular Physics — Mechanics, E&M & Beyond

> **Ultimate Goal:** Build a seamless, complete command of the **SAT Subject Test in Physics** (a.k.a. "SAT Regular Physics") curriculum — the three pillars of **Mechanics**, **Electricity & Magnetism**, and the supporting topics of **Waves & Optics, Heat & Thermodynamics, Modern Physics, and Measurement** — so that any SAT Physics-style question is solved at exam speed with total confidence.
>
> **Method:** **Problems first, concepts later.** Every session opens with a calculation you cannot yet do — by the end, you can do 20+ problems at exam speed.
>
> **Primary Reference:** *Everything You Need to Ace Physics in One Big Fat Notebook* (Matthew Blackman) — **100% coverage**.
>
> **Secondary References:** College Board *SAT Subject Test Physics* description, Barron's *SAT Subject Test Physics*, Princeton Review *Cracking the SAT Physics Subject Test*.
>
> **Drill Structure:** Every session = 5~8 worked examples + **10 Basic Drills** + **10 Advanced Drills** = exam-level fluency.
>
> **Scale:** ~24 sessions, approximately 50 hours. ~480 total drill problems across the curriculum.
>
> **Duration:** 90–150 minutes per session (50% worked examples, 50% drills).

## SAT Regular Physics — Content Breakdown

The official SAT Subject Test in Physics weights each domain as follows. This curriculum covers **100%** of them, organized into three parts:

| Part | Official Weight | Sessions |
|:-----|:---------------:|:--------:|
| **1. Mechanics** | 36–42% | 01–09 |
| **2. Electricity & Magnetism** | 18–24% | 10–15 |
| **3. Others** — Waves & Optics · Heat & Thermodynamics · Modern Physics · Measurement & History | ~40% (see below) | 16–24 |

Breakdown of Part 3 (Others):

| Domain | Official Weight | Sessions |
|:-------|:---------------:|:--------:|
| Waves & Optics | 15–19% | 16–18 |
| Heat & Thermodynamics | 6–11% | 19–20 |
| Modern Physics | 6–11% | 21–23 |
| General, History of Physics & Measurement | 9–14% | 24 (+ everywhere) |

> **Why this ordering?** Mechanics is the largest single block and the language in which every other domain is expressed (energy, momentum, and fields all reuse mechanics). E&M follows because it is the most calculation-heavy domain after mechanics. Part 3 rounds out the test — nearly half the SAT Physics score hides in waves, heat, modern, and measurement, so it is **not** an afterthought.

## Course Philosophy: The Number Is the Answer

The entire curriculum follows this progression:

```
Part 1: MECHANICS       → Kinematics, forces, energy, momentum, rotation, SHM (calculation first)
Part 2: E&M             → Fields, potential, circuits, magnetism, induction (calculation + interpretation)
Part 3: OTHERS          → Waves, heat, modern, measurement (qualitative + calculation, SAT-trap heavy)
```

### Learning Mode Shift Across Parts

```
Part 1: CALCULATION FIRST
  "Do the math. Speed and accuracy are the only goals."
  → If you can't calculate it, you haven't learned it.

Part 2: CALCULATION + INTERPRETATION
  "Calculate, then say what the field/circuit/current is doing."
  → Direction, sign, and 'which way' matter as much as the number.

Part 3: QUALITATIVE + CALCULATION
  "SAT loves qualitative traps here — read the graph, spot the misconception."
  → Wave phase, sign conventions, and unit checks are the real exam skills.
```

> **In Part 1, a student who can calculate but can't explain has succeeded.**
>
> **In Part 3, a student who can explain but can't dodge the classic trap has failed.**
>
> **The bar shifts from pure calculation speed to calculation + trap awareness.**

# PART 1: Mechanics — The Language of Motion

> **Core Question:** "How do things move, and why do they move that way?"
> **Coverage:** Kinematics, Newton's laws, circular motion & gravity, work/energy, momentum, rotation, SHM.
> **Theme:** Calculation-first. Every mechanics idea ends in a number with units. No concepts without numbers.

| # | Topic | Opening Problem | Core Skills | Time |
|:-:|-------|-----------------|-------------|:----:|
| **01** | **One-Dimensional Kinematics** | *A car accelerates from rest at 2.0 m/s² for 8.0 s, then brakes at 4.0 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?* | ① Displacement, velocity, acceleration ② Kinematic equations: $v=v_0+at$, $x=x_0+v_0t+\frac12at^2$, $v^2=v_0^2+2a\Delta x$ ③ Free fall with $g=9.8$ m/s² ④ Position–velocity–acceleration graphs & slopes | 120m |
| **02** | **Vectors & Two-Dimensional Kinematics** | *A projectile is launched at 30° above horizontal at 40 m/s from the edge of a 50 m cliff. How far from the base does it land?* | ① Vector components & addition ② Projectile motion: horizontal & vertical independence ③ Range, max height, flight time ④ Relative motion | 120m |
| **03** | **Newton's Laws & Dynamics** | *A 5.0 kg block rests on a 30° incline ($\mu_s=0.60$, $\mu_k=0.40$). Does it slide? If a 20 N force pushes it up the incline, what is the acceleration?* | ① Newton's three laws ② Free-body diagrams ③ Friction: $f_s \le \mu_s N$, $f_k = \mu_k N$ ④ Inclined planes, tension, pulleys ⑤ Apparent weight & elevators | 150m |

(Earth: $M=5.97\times10^{24}$ kg, $R=6.37\times10^6$ m)* | ① Uniform circular motion: $a_c=v^2/r$, $a_c=\omega^2r$ ② Centripetal force ③ Universal gravitation: $F=Gm_1m_2/r^2$ ④ Orbital motion & satellites ⑤ Kepler's laws (qualitative) | 120m |
| **05** | **Work, Energy & Power** | *A 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s brakes to a stop over 50 m. What average braking force? If the engine delivers 40 kW, what is the max steady speed up a 5° hill?* | ① Work: $W=Fd\cos\theta$ ② Kinetic energy: $KE=\frac12mv^2$ ③ Work–energy theorem ④ Potential energy (gravitational & spring) ⑤ Conservation of mechanical energy ⑥ Power: $P=W/t=Fv$ | 150m |

| **08** | **Simple Harmonic Motion & Oscillations** | *A 0.50 kg mass on a spring ($k=200$ N/m) is pulled 5.0 cm from equilibrium and released. Find the period, max speed, and total energy. | **09** | **Mechanics Capstone** | *A 2.0 kg block slides down a frictionless 3.0 m ramp (35°), hits a spring ($k=400$ N/m) at the bottom. (a) Max spring compression? (c) The block leaves a cliff at 8.0 m/s — range?* | ① Multi-concept integration (energy → momentum → projectile) ② SAT-style time management ③ Common trap patterns & unit checks | 150m |

## How to Use This Curriculum

### For Each Session:

1. **Open the session file.** Attack the Opening Problem. Get stuck.
2. **Work through the worked examples.** The pattern reveals itself through numbers.
3. **Read the Common Trap.** Watch someone else make the mistake — so you won't.
4. **Extract the 3-Step Procedure.** Write it down. Memorize it.
5. **Do 10 Basic Drills.** Speed and fluency. Under 2 minutes each by the end.
6. **Do 10 Advanced Drills.** Exam readiness. Multi-step. Time pressure.
7. **Read the Big Fat Notebook chapter.** Concepts lock into place.
8. **Check the formula sheet.** Every session should leave you able to reproduce its formulas from memory in under a minute.

### The Three-Part Exam Strategy:

```
Part 1 (Mechanics):  Your score foundation. Master the calculation templates — 
                     if you can do every session's drills at speed, you bank ~40% of the test.
```

## The Identity of This Curriculum — v1

```
What sets this curriculum apart:

1. CALCULATION FIRST (Part 1):
   Open every topic with a problem the learner cannot yet solve.
   Work through it with numbers. Extract the pattern. Automate with drills.
   Only then read the concept chapter.
   No explaining required yet — just fast, accurate calculation.

2. CALCULATION + INTERPRETATION (Part 2):
   Fields, potentials, circuits, and induction demand both the number AND
   the direction. "3.0 N" is only half an answer; "toward the charge" completes it.
   "Which way does current flow?" is a legitimate SAT question.

3. QUALITATIVE + TRAP AWARENESS (Part 3):
   Waves, heat, modern, and measurement carry the SAT's favorite qualitative traps:
   phase vs. path difference, sign conventions, rest vs. moving frames,
   and "more precise ≠ more accurate."
   If you can dodge the trap, you've learned it.

4. SYNTHESIS (Sessions 09, 15, 24):
   Every part ends with a capstone that forces multi-concept integration —
   the exact skill the SAT's hardest questions demand.
```

2     | Calculate + Interpret     | Direction & sign    | Can you calculate AND say which way?
3     | Qualitative + Calculate   | Spot the trap       | Can you dodge the misconception AND compute?
```

24 sessions x 20 drills = 480 problems.
Part 1:  ~180 drill problems (pure calculation fluency)
```
