## Problem 1 — Beats, Horns, and Echoes *(Doppler + beats)*

Two tuning forks: $440$ Hz and $443$ Hz. ($v_{sound}=343$ m/s)

**(a)** Beat frequency when both are stationary.

**(b)** The $440$ Hz fork moves toward you at $10$ m/s. What frequency do you hear, and what beat frequency results with the stationary $443$ Hz fork?

**(c)** A train at $25$ m/s sounds its $500$ Hz horn as it approaches a **wall** (the horn reflects off the wall). Find the frequency of the echo the **engineer** hears, and the beat frequency between the horn and its echo.

## Problem 2 — The Fish and the Mirror-Surface *(total internal reflection)*

Light travels in water ($n=1.33$) and strikes the water–air surface at $60°$.

**(a)** Does it refract out, or totally reflect? Compute.

**(b)** What is the largest angle of incidence (in water) for which light still escapes into air?

**(c)** A fish at depth $1.0$ m looks up. What is the radius of the "sky window" on the surface (the circle through which it sees the outside world)?

**(d)** The fish swims down to $2.0$ m. Does the cone of its view widen? Does the window radius change? Explain.

## Problem 3 — The Grating That Separates Colors *(diffraction)*

A diffraction grating has $6000$ lines/cm. White light ($400$–$700$ nm) shines on it.

**(a)** Grating spacing $d$.

**(b)** First-order angles for violet ($400$ nm) and red ($700$ nm).

**(c)** What is the **highest order** in which red light appears? (Check $\sin\theta\le1$.) How many complete orders fit for red?

**(d)** The second-order violet sits at what angle? Could second-order violet ever *overlap* first-order red? Use your numbers — and state the general overlap condition.

## Problem 4 — The Engine That Thinks *(Carnot, refrigerator, staging)*

**(a)** A Carnot engine runs between $600$ K and $300$ K, absorbing $1000$ J per cycle. Find work output and heat rejected.

**(b)** The same machine runs in reverse as a refrigerator ($300\to600$ K) driven by $200$ J of work. How much heat is removed from the cold reservoir, and how much is dumped into the hot one?

**(c)** Two Carnot engines in series: engine 1 between $600$ K and $450$ K, engine 2 between $450$ K and $300$ K. Show that the total efficiency equals that of a single Carnot engine between $600$ K and $300$ K. What does this suggest about "staging" engines?

**(d)** Why do real engines fall short of Carnot? Name the main irreversibilities.

## Problem 5 — The Muon That Shouldn't Arrive *(special relativity)*

Muons are created at the top of the atmosphere (~$10$ km altitude) with speed $0.995c$ toward the ground. Their **proper** (rest-frame) half-life is $2.2\,\mu$s.

**(a)** Classically (no relativity): how far does a muon travel in one half-life? Does it reach the ground?

**(b)** Relativistically: what is the Lorentz factor $\gamma$, and the dilated half-life in the Earth frame? How far does it travel in that dilated half-life?

**(c)** What fraction of a muon beam survives the full $10$ km journey? (Use $N/N_0=(1/2)^{t/T_{1/2}}$ with Earth-frame quantities.)

**(d)** Viewing from the muon's frame: how thick is the atmosphere, and how does the muon "explain" reaching the ground? (Both frames must agree — that's the point.)
