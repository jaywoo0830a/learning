## Problem 1 — The Speaker's Sphere

A speaker radiates $1.0$ W uniformly in all directions. Threshold $I_0=10^{-12}$ W/m².

**(a)** Intensity at $5.0$ m.

**(b)** Sound level in dB at $5.0$ m.

**(c)** Distance at which the level is $80$ dB.

**(d)** Two *identical, coherent, in-phase* speakers: at a point where the path difference is exactly one wavelength, how many **dB** is the level above one speaker alone? What would happen with two *incoherent* speakers (powers add)? This is the difference between interference and simple addition.

## Problem 2 — Lens, Then Mirror

An object is $12$ cm from a converging lens ($f=8.0$ cm). A plane mirror stands $20$ cm **beyond** the lens, facing it.

**(a)** Find the image produced by the lens alone: position, nature, magnification. Where is it relative to the mirror?

**(b)** Light now reflects off the plane mirror and passes back **through the lens** toward an eye on the object side. Locate the **final image**. Is it real or virtual? Upright or inverted? What is the total magnification?

## Problem 3 — The Double Slit Goes Swimming

Double slit: spacing $d=0.20$ mm, screen $2.0$ m away, wavelength $550$ nm.

**(a)** Fringe spacing on the screen.

**(b)** The whole apparatus is immersed in water ($n=1.33$): new fringe spacing.

**(c)** If instead the slits are moved to $0.10$ mm apart (in air): new fringe spacing.

**(d)** A thin transparent film is placed over **one** slit so the light passing through it is delayed by exactly **half a wavelength**. Describe the new pattern. What happens to the central maximum? What if the delay is a full wavelength?

## Problem 4 — Steam Into the Cup

Steam at $100°$C is bubbled into $0.40$ kg of water at $20°$C in an insulated container until the water reaches $50°$C. ($c_w=4186$ J/kg·K; $L_v=2.26\times10^6$ J/kg)

**(a)** Mass of steam condensed.

**(b)** What mass of steam would bring the water to exactly $100°$C?

**(c)** Can you ever get the water **above** $100°$C this way? Explain what happens once the mixture reaches $100°$C.

## Problem 5 — Carbon in the Artifact

Carbon-14 has a half-life of $5730$ years. A living organism shows $0.23$ Bq per gram of carbon.

**(a)** An ancient wooden artifact shows $0.0575$ Bq/g. Estimate its age.

**(b)** Write the beta-decay equation for carbon-14.

**(c)** Compute the energy released, given masses: $^{14}$C $=14.003242$ u, $^{14}$N $=14.003074$ u, electron $=0.000549$ u. ($1$ u $=931.5$ MeV/c²)

**(d)** Explain why beta emitters can be hazardous **outside** the body while alpha emitters are mainly dangerous when inhaled or ingested.
