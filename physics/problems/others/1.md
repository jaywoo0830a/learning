## Problem 1 — The String That Sings

A string of length $1.5$ m and mass $3.0$ g is under tension $T$. Its fundamental frequency is $80$ Hz.

**(a)** Find the wave speed on the string.

**(b)** Find the tension.

**(c)** Find the 3rd harmonic frequency.

**(d)** The string is shortened to $1.0$ m (same tension). New fundamental?

**(e)** The string now vibrates at the 3rd harmonic (240 Hz). A listener approaches the source at $20$ m/s. What frequency does she hear? ($v_{sound}=343$ m/s)

## Problem 2 — The Fish, the Bird, and the Window

A fish is $1.0$ m below the surface of a pond ($n=1.33$). A bird hovers $2.0$ m above the surface.

**(a)** How deep does the fish *appear* to the bird?

**(b)** How high above the surface does the bird *appear* to the fish?

**(c)** Find the critical angle for light going water → air. What happens to the fish's view of the sky at angles beyond it? (This is the "Snell's window.")

**(d)** The fish looks up and sees the sky in a cone. If the fish is at depth $1.0$ m, what is the radius of the "window" on the surface through which it sees the outside world?

## Problem 3 — The Ice Cube With an Attitude

A $0.10$ kg ice cube at $-10°$C is dropped into $0.50$ kg of water at $30°$C in an insulated container. ($c_{ice}=2100$, $c_w=4186$ J/kg·K; $L_f=3.34\times10^5$ J/kg)

**(a)** Determine the final state — does all the ice melt, or does some remain? Show the comparison that lets you decide.

**(b)** Find the final temperature.

**(c)** Repeat with $0.30$ kg of ice: what is the final temperature now, and how much ice remains?

**(d)** State the general decision procedure (when to check what) so you never guess a final state again.

## Problem 4 — The Rectangle Engine

$n=2.0$ mol of monatomic ideal gas runs a rectangular cycle: A$(1\,\text{atm},20\,\text{L})$ → B$(1\,\text{atm},60\,\text{L})$ → C$(3\,\text{atm},60\,\text{L})$ → D$(3\,\text{atm},20\,\text{L})$ → A. ($1$ atm·L $=101.3$ J; $R=0.0821$ L·atm/mol·K)

**(a)** Temperature at each corner.

**(b)** Net work done **by** the gas per cycle (area of the rectangle).

**(c)** Heat **added** to the gas during the cycle.

**(d)** Thermal efficiency (work / heat input).

**(e)** Compare with a Carnot engine between the hottest and coldest corner temperatures — why is the rectangle so much worse?

## Problem 5 — The Photon That Kicks an Electron

Light of wavelength $250$ nm shines on sodium (work function $\phi=2.28$ eV). ($hc=1240$ eV·nm)

**(a)** Maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons, and the stopping voltage.

**(b)** The light intensity doubles. What changes — the electrons' energy, their number, or both?

**(c)** Find the de Broglie wavelength of the most energetic photoelectron.

**(d)** What is the **longest** wavelength that still ejects electrons?
