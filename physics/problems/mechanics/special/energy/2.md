# Mechanics — Special: Energy — The Bank Ledger

> **Purpose:** Five problems that train the bank-ledger model from the **energy** side — watching the Balance stay constant while Cash and Deposit swap.
> **The model:** each object holds its own **Cash** (= kinetic energy). The objects in a system share a joint **Deposit** (= potential energy — owned by the *pair/system*, never by one object alone). The **Current Balance** = total Cash + Deposit = the system's total mechanical energy.
> **The rules:** Cash ↔ Deposit transfers are *internal* — the Balance never changes. The Balance changes only through an **external transaction**: Balance went **up** → something *outside* did work **on** the system; Balance went **down** → the system did work **on** something outside. Friction is a **fee**: a one-way withdrawal that leaves the ledger as heat.
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus.**
> **How to use:** Solve the folded sub-problems in order. Full solutions (✅ Solution) live in [`solutions/2-solutions.md`](solutions/2-solutions.md) — open a problem's solution only after finishing it.
> **The core feel to build:** the ledger **always balances**. Energy never disappears — it moves between Cash and Deposit, or leaves through a non-refundable fee.

## Problem 1 — The Ledger Never Lies

A $0.5$ kg ball is thrown straight up at $20$ m/s from ground level. Take $g=10$ m/s². System = ball + Earth.

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

At height $10$ m, does the ledger tell you whether the ball is going up or down? What piece of information does the ledger **not** keep — and where does that information live instead?

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Between launch and the top, how much did the Deposit change and where did the Cash go? Did anything **outside** the system do work during the whole flight? Justify from the Balance column alone.

## Problem 2 — Who Owns the Deposit?

A $2.0$ kg block **A** and a $3.0$ kg block **B** rest on a frictionless table, pressed against a compressed spring ($k=600$ N/m, compressed $x=0.50$ m). The spring is released and the blocks fly apart.

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

How much energy is stored in the spring? Whose money is it — A's, B's, or someone else's? (Think in terms of joint ownership of the Deposit.)

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

When the spring has relaxed, all the Deposit has become the blocks' Cash. Use **momentum conservation and energy conservation together** to find the speed of each block.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

How is the $75$ J split between A and B? Is it $50/50$? Why does the lighter block get more Cash — and why did **neither** block ever own $37.5$ J?

## Problem 3 — The Non-Refundable Fee

A $2.0$ kg cart starts from rest at the top of a rough ramp of height $5.0$ m. Friction takes a $20$ J **fee** during the descent. Take $g=10$ m/s². System = cart + Earth.

<details>
<summary><b>3.1</b></summary>

Read the ledger: starting Balance, the fee, and the final Cash. Find the cart's speed at the bottom.

<details>
<summary><b>3.2</b></summary>

Where is the $20$ J now? Is the sentence "energy is conserved" still true even though the Balance dropped from $100$ J to $80$ J? Explain how both can be true at once.

<details>
<summary><b>3.3</b></summary>

You now want to return the cart to the top. If you push it back **up the same rough ramp**, how much work must you do? (Careful: the fee is charged *again* on the way up.) How much would it cost if you lifted it straight up instead? What does this say about fees and round trips?

## Problem 4 — Two Ledgers, One Slide

<details>
<summary><b>4.1</b></summary>

<details>
<summary><b>4.2</b></summary>

Use **system B = skier alone** (Earth is outside). Read the Balance at the top and at the bottom. Did the Balance change — and if so, who did work on the system, and how much?

A friend is confused: "So did gravity do work on the skier, or not?" Give the one-line answer, and the reconciliation equation connecting the two ledgers: $W_{\text{gravity (skier-only ledger)}} = -\Delta U_{\text{(joint ledger)}}$. Why can you use either ledger but never both in the same equation?

## Problem 5 — Audit the Statements

A pendulum swings down from height $h$, through its lowest point, and (in an ideal world) rises to height $h$ on the other side.

<details>
<summary><b>5.1</b></summary>

Rank the Balance at the three moments: release point, lowest point, other-side turning point.

<details>
<summary><b>5.2</b></summary>

Which statements are wrong, and why?
- (a) "The pendulum's kinetic energy is conserved."
- (b) "Energy is conserved."
- (c) "At the lowest point, all the energy is Cash."
- (d) "The Deposit is largest at the lowest point."

<details>
<summary><b>5.3</b></summary>

Now air resistance is present, and the bob only rises to $0.8h$ on the other side. How big was the **fee**? In the teacher's words: the Balance *dropped* — so who did work on whom, and where exactly is the missing money now?
