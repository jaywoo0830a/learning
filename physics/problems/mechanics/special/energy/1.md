# Mechanics — Special: Energy — Feel Builder

> **Purpose:** Five intuition-building problems on **energy conservation** ($E_i=E_f$) before harder drills.
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus.**
> **How to use:** Solve the folded sub-problems in order. Full solutions (✅ Solution) live in [`solutions/1-solutions.md`](solutions/1-solutions.md) — open a problem's solution only after finishing it.
> **The core feel to build:** energy is conserved but keeps **changing form** — track it with an energy ledger; when friction appears, energy is still conserved, but some of it leaves as heat.

---

## Problem 1 — The Roller Coaster That Never Runs Out

A $2.0$ kg cart starts from rest at height $H=20$ m on a **frictionless** track. Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Find its speed when it passes a point $5.0$ m above the ground.

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Now the track has friction, and the cart reaches the $5.0$ m point with speed $12$ m/s instead. How much mechanical energy was lost to friction?

## Problem 2 — The Energy Ledger

A $2.0$ kg block slides from rest down a **frictionless** hill of height $5.0$ m, reaches level ground, and compresses a spring ($k=500$ N/m). Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

Find the block's speed at the bottom of the hill.

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

Find the maximum compression of the spring.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

Build the full **energy ledger** — PE, KE, spring PE — at (i) the top, (ii) the bottom of the hill, (iii) maximum compression. Then suppose the level ground is **rough** ($\mu_k=0.20$) for $3.0$ m before the spring: how much energy is lost to friction, and what is the new compression?
