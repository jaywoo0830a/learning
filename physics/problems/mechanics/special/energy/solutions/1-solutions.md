## Problem 1 — The Roller Coaster That Never Runs Out

**1.1 —** Height dropped $=20-5=15$ m. Energy conservation:
$$mgh_{\rm top}=\tfrac12mv^2+mgh \Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2g(H-h)}=\sqrt{2(10)(15)}=\sqrt{300}\approx17.3\text{ m/s}.$$

**1.2 —** No. Both KE and PE are proportional to $m$, so $m$ cancels in $\tfrac12mv^2=mgh$. **Mass never appears in a frictionless energy-conservation result** — a feather and a boulder roll down a frictionless hill at the same speed.

**1.3 —** Ideal KE at that point: $\tfrac12(2)(300)=300$ J. Actual: $\tfrac12(2)(144)=144$ J. **Lost: $156$ J** — that energy left the system as heat (and a little sound) through friction.

> **The feel:** on a frictionless track, energy just swaps between PE and KE forever — that's why the roller coaster "never runs out". Friction is the thief: it quietly converts mechanical energy into heat.

## Problem 2 — The Energy Ledger

**2.1 —** $v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2(10)(5)}=10$ m/s.

**2.2 —** $\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12kx^2 \Rightarrow x=v\sqrt{m/k}=10\sqrt{2/500}=\sqrt{0.4}\approx0.63$ m.

**2.3 — Ledger** (total stays $100$ J):
| Stage | PE | KE | Spring PE | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) top of hill | $100$ J | $0$ | $0$ | $100$ J |
| (ii) bottom of hill | $0$ | $100$ J | $0$ | $100$ J |
| (iii) max compression | $0$ | $0$ | $100$ J | $100$ J |

- **With friction:** $W_f=\mu_k mg\,d=0.2(2)(10)(3)=12$ J leaves as heat. Remaining for the spring: $100-12=88$ J $\Rightarrow x=\sqrt{2(88)/500}=\sqrt{0.352}\approx0.59$ m.
- Note the ledger still balances: $88$ J (spring) $+12$ J (heat) $=100$ J.

> **The feel:** the energy **ledger** is the whole game — every stage, the same total, just in different pockets. Friction doesn't destroy energy; it moves it into a pocket (heat) you can't get back.

## Problem 3 — The Pendulum's Favorite Trick

**3.1 —** Height drop: $h=L(1-\cos60°)=2(0.5)=1.0$ m.
$$v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{20}\approx4.5\text{ m/s}.$$

**3.2 —** Radial $F=ma$ at the bottom: $T-mg=mv^2/L$.
$$T=mg+\frac{mv^2}{L}=10+\frac{1(20)}{2}=20\text{ N}=2mg.$$
The string must support the weight **plus** the centripetal demand.

**3.3 —** $h=L=2.0$ m, $v=\sqrt{40}\approx6.3$ m/s, $T=mg+\frac{mv^2}{L}=10+\frac{40}{2}=30\text{ N}=3mg$.

- **Pattern:** $T_{\rm bottom}=mg(3-2\cos\theta_0)$. For $60°$: $mg(3-1)=2mg$ ✓. For $90°$: $mg(3-0)=3mg$ ✓. For $0°$ (released at the bottom): $mg$ ✓.

> **The feel:** energy alone gives you the *speed*, but to find the *tension* you must add Newton's second law. The apparent weight at the bottom is always **more** than $mg$ — that's the "pulled into the seat" feeling on a swing or roller coaster.

## Problem 4 — The Pendulum That "Runs Out"

**4.1 —** Speed is greatest at the **bottom** (all PE converted to KE): $v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{20}\approx4.5$ m/s.

**4.2 —** Initial ME $=mgh=10m$ J. After losing $10\%$: ME $=9m$ J. At the other side all ME is PE: $mgh'=9m\Rightarrow h'=0.9$ m. The missing $10\%$ left as **heat** (air resistance, pivot friction) and a little sound.

**4.3 —** Energy is **never destroyed**. The pendulum's *mechanical* energy decreases, but the total (mechanical + heat in the air and surroundings) is conserved. "Runs out of energy" is wrong — it runs out of *usable mechanical* energy, which has been spread into the environment as heat.

> **The misconception fixed:** a slowing pendulum is not losing energy from the universe — it's converting ordered mechanical energy into disordered heat. Total energy is always conserved.

## Problem 5 — The Loop's Weightless Moment

**5.1 —** Height dropped from start to top: $h-2R=5R-2R=3R=6$ m. Energy: $\tfrac12mv^2=mg(3R)$ → $v^2=2g(3R)=2(10)(6)=120$ → $v\approx11.0$ m/s.

**5.2 —** At the top, both forces point down: $N+mg=mv^2/R$ → $N=mv^2/R-mg=2(120)/2-20=120-20=100$ N (downward on the cart). The track pushes **down** on the cart, so the rider is pressed **into the seat** — feeling *heavier*, not weightless.

**5.3 —** Weightlessness is set by the **normal force** ($N=0$), not by energy. Set $N=0$: $mv^2/R=mg$ → $v^2=gR$. Energy gives $v^2=2g(h-2R)$, so $gR=2g(h-2R)$ → $h=2.5R=5.0$ m. Starting below $5$ m, the cart leaves the track before the top.

> **The misconception fixed:** "energy runs low at the top" is not why you feel light. Energy sets the *speed*; the *feel* is set by Newton's second law — weightlessness is the precise moment $N=0$.
