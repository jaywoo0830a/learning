# Mechanics — Special: Work — The Bank Ledger

> **Purpose:** Five problems that train one mental model for **work at the system level**: the bank ledger.
> **The model:** each object holds its own **Cash** (= kinetic energy). The objects in a system share a joint **Deposit** (= potential energy — owned by the *pair/system*, never by one object alone). The **Current Balance** = total Cash + Deposit = the system's total mechanical energy.
> **The rules:** Cash ↔ Deposit transfers are *internal* — the Balance never changes. The Balance changes only through an **external transaction**: Balance went **up** → something *outside* did work **on** the system; Balance went **down** → the system did work **on** something outside.
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus.**
> **How to use:** Solve the folded sub-problems in order. Full solutions (✅ Solution) live in [`solutions/2-solutions.md`](solutions/2-solutions.md) — open a problem's solution only after finishing it.
> **The core feel to build:** before computing anything, ask *"whose ledger is this, and which transactions are internal vs external?"* — that one question decides whether work is being done, and by whom.

## Problem 1 — Cash, Deposit, or Balance?

The ledger only helps if every entry is classified correctly. Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Classify each underlined quantity as **Cash** (kinetic energy), **Deposit** (potential energy), part of the **Balance** (total mechanical energy), or **outside the ledger**:
- (a) a $60$ kg skier gliding at $12$ m/s — her energy of motion,
- (b) a $2$ kg book resting on a shelf $3$ m above the floor — its $mgh$,
- (c) a stretched slingshot band,
- (d) the heat produced by brake pads stopping a car,
- (e) a pendulum swinging through its lowest point, vs. hanging at its highest point.

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

A $0.5$ kg ball is released from rest at height $4.0$ m. Build the full ledger — **Cash, Deposit, Balance** — at three moments: (i) the instant of release, (ii) passing $h=2.0$ m on the way down, (iii) an instant before hitting the ground. What does the unchanging Balance tell you about external work?

<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

A friend says: "The ball at height $4$ m **has** $20$ J of potential energy." Is that statement precisely right? Who actually co-owns the Deposit, and why does the ball end up with (almost) all the Cash when the Deposit converts?

## Problem 2 — Reading the Balance

A $2.0$ kg box hangs from a cable. Take $g=10$ m/s², and always put the **Earth inside the system** (system = box + Earth).

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

The cable lifts the box straight up $3.0$ m from rest to rest. Read the ledger: how much did the Balance change? Which outside agent did work on the system, and how much? Cross-check your reading by computing the cable's work directly from force × distance.

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

This time the box ends the $3.0$ m lift moving upward at $2.0$ m/s. Read the ledger again — how much of the cable's work went into the Deposit, and how much into Cash? Verify with kinematics ($a = v^2/2d$).

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

The cable now **lowers** the same box $3.0$ m at constant speed. Read the Balance change: who did work on whom this time? Does gravity do any *separate* work in this ledger — or is its effect already counted somewhere?

<details>

## Problem 4 — The Fee

A $4.0$ kg block slides from rest $5.0$ m down a $30°$ incline with $\mu_k=0.30$. Take $g=10$ m/s². System = block + Earth.

<details>
<summary><b>4.1</b></summary>

Compute the starting Deposit. Compute the **fee** (the work done by friction). Then read the ledger at the bottom: what is the final Cash, and the block's speed? By how much did the Balance drop, and where did that money go?

<details>
<summary><b>4.2</b></summary>

Is the fee **refundable** — can that money ever come back into the ledger on its own? Why does this matter for reading the Balance?

<details>
<summary><b>4.3</b></summary>

Suppose instead the incline is frictionless. Which external agent changes the Balance now — the normal force or friction? Compute the final speed and compare with 4.1.

## Problem 5 — Auditing Wrong Ledgers

Three student statements. For each, rebuild the correct ledger. Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>5.1</b></summary>

For a $2$ kg ball falling $4.0$ m, a student writes: "gravity does $+80$ J of work, potential energy changes by $-80$ J, and kinetic energy changes by $+80$ J — so the ball gains $80$ J of energy in total." Find the double counting, and show the correct ledger(s).

<details>
<summary><b>5.2</b></summary>

For the box being lifted at **constant speed** (Problem 2.1), a student says: "The net force on the box is zero, so no work is done, so nothing's energy changed." What is true in that sentence, and what does it miss about the *system's* Balance?

<details>
<summary><b>5.3</b></summary>

"Normal forces never do work." A $60$ kg passenger stands in an elevator that rises $10$ m at constant speed. Does the floor's normal force do work on the passenger? Explain the correct rule — and read the Balance for both systems: (i) passenger alone, (ii) passenger + Earth. Which outside agent deposited the money?
