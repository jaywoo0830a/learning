# Mechanics — Special: Work — Feel Builder

> **Purpose:** Five intuition-building problems on **work** ($W=Fd\cos\theta$) before harder drills.
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus.**
> **How to use:** Solve the folded sub-problems in order. Full solutions (✅ Solution) live in [`solutions/1-solutions.md`](solutions/1-solutions.md) — open a problem's solution only after finishing it.
> **The core feel to build:** work is done only by the *component of force along the displacement*; perpendicular forces do zero work; **net work = ΔKE**.

---

## Problem 1 — The Case of the Missing Work

A $5.0$ kg block slides $3.0$ m down a **frictionless** incline at $30°$. Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Identify every force on the block. Compute the work done by **each** force over the $3.0$ m slide, then the net work.

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

The same block is pushed at **constant speed** $2.0$ m across a rough horizontal floor ($\mu_k=0.40$) by a horizontal force. Find the applied force, the work done by each force, and the net work. What does net work $=0$ imply about kinetic energy?

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

A $2.0$ kg ball is thrown straight up $5.0$ m and falls back to the starting point. Compute the total work done by gravity on the **round trip**. What does this show about gravity's work and the path?

## Problem 2 — Work From a Graph

A spring ($k=200$ N/m) is stretched. The force it pulls back with is $F=kx$.

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

Draw (or sketch) the $F$–$x$ graph for the spring. Use the **area under the graph** to find the work you do stretching it from $x=0$ to $x=0.20$ m.

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

Verify your answer with the formula $\frac12 kx^2$. Then find the work needed to stretch it from $x=0.10$ m to $x=0.20$ m (a trapezoid — not a full triangle).

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

A different force varies as $F=30x$ N ($x$ in m). Find the work done from $x=0$ to $x=2.0$ m using the same area idea.

## Problem 3 — The Marathon of Lifting

You lift a $10$ kg box. Take $g=10$ m/s².

<details>
<summary><b>3.1</b></summary>

Lift the box straight up $2.0$ m at **constant speed**. Compute the work **you** do, the work gravity does, and the net work.

<details>
<summary><b>3.2</b></summary>

You now **carry** the same box $5.0$ m across a level floor at constant speed. Compute the work done by the upward force you exert and by gravity. Why does it feel tiring if no mechanical work is done on the box?

<details>
<summary><b>3.3</b></summary>

Compare lifting the box straight up $2.0$ m with pulling it up a **frictionless ramp** $4.0$ m long (same height). Find the work and the required force in each case. Then find the work if the ramp has friction ($\mu_k=0.20$).
