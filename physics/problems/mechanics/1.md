# Mechanics — Day 1

> **Mix:** 1-D kinematics · Newton's laws (pulley + incline) · Work & energy (friction + spring) · Momentum + SHM · Circular motion + energy
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus**.
> **How to use:** Draw a diagram first. Name the principle before each step. Keep units everywhere. Solve symbolically, then plug numbers. Check answers only when finished.
> **Deliberately hint-free:** the principle and strategy for each problem are *not* stated — recalling them is part of the exercise.

## Problem 1 — Two Balls in the Air

A ball **A** is dropped from rest from the top of a tower of height $H = 45$ m. At the **same instant**, ball **B** is thrown straight upward from the ground with exactly the speed needed to reach the top of the tower.

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Find the initial speed $v_0$ of ball B.

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

At what time, and at what height above the ground, do the two balls pass each other?

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Compute the *relative speed* of the two balls at the moment they pass. Write your expression without plugging in numbers. Does it depend on time? Interpret your result, and explain why the two balls' relative motion behaves this way.

## Problem 2 — The Stubborn Crate on the Incline

A $m_1=4.0$ kg crate sits on a $30°$ incline ($\mu_s=0.50$, $\mu_k=0.30$), connected by a light string over a frictionless pulley to a hanging $m_2=5.0$ kg block.

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

Does the system move? If so, in which direction, and with what acceleration?

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

Find the tension in the string while moving.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

Find the **range of $m_2$** for which the system stays at rest (static friction can act either way). Is $m_2=5.0$ kg inside that range?

</details>

## Problem 3 — Down the Hill, Into the Spring

A $2.0$ kg block starts from rest at height $h$ on a **frictionless** incline, slides onto a **rough horizontal** stretch of length $L=1.5$ m ($\mu_k=0.40$), and then compresses a spring ($k=500$ N/m) by $x=0.30$ m before momentarily stopping.

<details>
<summary><b>3.1</b></summary>

Find $h$.

<details>
<summary><b>3.2</b></summary>

The spring now pushes the block back. How high up the incline does it climb before stopping?

<details>
<summary><b>3.3</b></summary>

If the horizontal stretch were frictionless instead, how high would $h$ need to be to produce the same $0.30$ m compression — and how high would the block return? Explain the difference.

</details>

## Problem 4 — Bullet Into a Spring-Launcher

<details>

Find the speed of block+bullet immediately after the collision.

<details>
<summary><b>4.2</b></summary>

<details>
<summary><b>4.3</b></summary>

How long after the collision does the block reach maximum compression?

<details>

What fraction of the bullet's original kinetic energy is lost in the collision? Where does it go?

## Problem 5 — The Loop That Is (Almost) Too Small

A $500$ kg roller-coaster car enters a vertical loop of radius $R=8.0$ m. Treat the track as frictionless.

<details>
<summary><b>5.1</b></summary>

Find the minimum speed at the **bottom** of the loop for the car to just barely complete the loop (contact maintained at the top).

<details>
<summary><b>5.2</b></summary>

If the car enters at $22$ m/s, find the normal force from the track at the bottom.

<details>
<summary><b>5.3</b></summary>

If the car enters at $18$ m/s, it is **too slow** to complete the loop. Find the angle from the bottom, the height above the bottom, and its speed at the point where it leaves the track.
