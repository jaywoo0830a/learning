## Problem 1 — Two Balls in the Air *(kinematics, relative motion)*

A ball **A** is dropped from rest from the top of a tower of height $H = 45$ m. At the **same instant**, ball **B** is thrown straight upward from the ground with exactly the speed needed to reach the top of the tower.

**(a)** Find the initial speed $v_0$ of ball B.

**(b)** At what time, and at what height above the ground, do the two balls pass each other?

**(c)** Compute the *relative speed* of the two balls at the moment they pass. Show — without plugging in numbers — that this relative speed is the same at **every** instant during the motion, and give its value in terms of $v_0$. Why does gravity cancel out of the relative motion?

## Problem 2 — The Stubborn Crate on the Incline *(Newton's laws, static vs. kinetic friction)*

A $m_1=4.0$ kg crate sits on a $30°$ incline ($\mu_s=0.50$, $\mu_k=0.30$), connected by a light string over a frictionless pulley to a hanging $m_2=5.0$ kg block.

**(a)** Does the system move? If so, in which direction, and with what acceleration?

**(b)** Find the tension in the string while moving.

**(c)** Find the **range of $m_2$** for which the system stays at rest (static friction can act either way). Is $m_2=5.0$ kg inside that range?

## Problem 3 — Down the Hill, Into the Spring *(work–energy with friction, two passes)*

A $2.0$ kg block starts from rest at height $h$ on a **frictionless** incline, slides onto a **rough horizontal** stretch of length $L=1.5$ m ($\mu_k=0.40$), and then compresses a spring ($k=500$ N/m) by $x=0.30$ m before momentarily stopping.

**(a)** Find $h$.

**(b)** The spring now pushes the block back. How high up the incline does it climb before stopping?

**(c)** If the horizontal stretch were frictionless instead, how high would $h$ need to be to produce the same $0.30$ m compression — and how high would the block return? Explain the difference.

## Problem 4 — Bullet Into a Spring-Launcher *(momentum + energy + SHM timing)*

A $0.020$ kg bullet at $400$ m/s embeds into a $1.98$ kg block at rest on a frictionless table. The block is attached to a light spring, $k=2000$ N/m.

**(a)** Find the speed of block+bullet immediately after the collision.

**(b)** Find the maximum compression of the spring.

**(c)** How long after the collision does the block reach maximum compression?

**(d)** What fraction of the bullet's original kinetic energy is lost in the collision? Where does it go?

## Problem 5 — The Loop That Is (Almost) Too Small *(circular motion + energy)*

A $500$ kg roller-coaster car enters a vertical loop of radius $R=8.0$ m. Treat the track as frictionless.

**(a)** Find the minimum speed at the **bottom** of the loop for the car to just barely complete the loop (contact maintained at the top).

**(b)** If the car enters at $22$ m/s, find the normal force from the track at the bottom.

**(c)** If the car enters at $18$ m/s, it is **too slow** to complete the loop. Find the angle from the bottom, the height above the bottom, and its speed at the point where it leaves the track. *(Hint: it leaves when the normal force drops to zero; combine the radial force equation with energy conservation.)*
