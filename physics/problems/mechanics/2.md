## Problem 1 — From the Cliff, Two Angles

A stone is thrown from the top of a $20$ m cliff at $25$ m/s. Take $\sin37°=0.60$, $\cos37°=0.80$ (complementary $53°$: $\sin53°=0.80$, $\cos53°=0.60$).

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Thrown at $37°$ above horizontal: find the time of flight and the horizontal range.

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

Find the speed and direction (angle below horizontal) at impact.

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Thrown at the complementary $53°$: find the range. Compare with (a). Is the familiar "same range for complementary angles" rule preserved? Explain.

<details>
<summary><b>1.4</b></summary>

Qualitatively: to maximize range from a cliff-top, do you launch above or below $45°$? Justify in one sentence.

</details>

## Problem 2 — The Crate That Won't Decide

A $10$ kg crate sits on a $25°$ incline ($\mu_s=0.45$, $\mu_k=0.30$; $\sin25°=0.42$, $\cos25°=0.91$). A force $F$ is applied **up** the incline.

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

With no force applied, does the crate slide on its own?

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

Find the minimum $F$ needed to keep it from sliding down.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

If $F=60$ N, does it slide up, slide down, or stay put? Find the friction force.

<details>
<summary><b>2.4</b></summary>

Find $F$ for the crate to move **up** at constant speed.

## Problem 3 — Collision With a Choice

A $3.0$ kg block slides down a frictionless track from height $2.0$ m and collides on a level, frictionless surface with a $1.0$ kg block at rest. Afterward, the $1.0$ kg block slides up a frictionless ramp.

<details>
<summary><b>3.1</b></summary>

Speed of the $3.0$ kg block just before the collision.

<details>
<summary><b>3.2</b></summary>

If the collision is perfectly **elastic**, find both speeds after, and the max height reached by the $1.0$ kg block.

<details>
<summary><b>3.3</b></summary>

If the collision is perfectly **inelastic** (they stick), find the max height. Compare with (b) and explain physically why it's lower.

## Problem 4 — The Falling Rod

A uniform rod (mass $3.0$ kg, length $L=2.0$ m) pivots without friction at one end. It is held horizontal and released.

<details>
<summary><b>4.1</b></summary>

Find the torque about the pivot just after release, and the angular acceleration. ($I=\tfrac13 mL^2$)

<details>
<summary><b>4.2</b></summary>

Find the angular speed as it passes through the vertical.

<details>
<summary><b>4.3</b></summary>

What is the speed of the **tip** at that instant? Compare it with the speed a point mass would have after falling freely through the same height $L$ — which is bigger, and why?

<details>

<details>
<summary><b>5.2</b></summary>

Find the period.

<details>

Find the total mechanical energy, and the speed when the mass is halfway between equilibrium and maximum displacement.

<details>
<summary><b>5.4</b></summary>

If the mass is doubled (same $k$, same amplitude), what happens to the period and the maximum speed? Explain.
