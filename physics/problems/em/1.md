## Problem 1 — Three Charges, One Balance Point

Two charges, $+q$ and $+4q$, sit $d=0.60$ m apart.

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

Where between them does a positive test charge feel zero net force? (Distance from $+q$.)

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

Would a **negative** test charge balance at the same spot? Explain.

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Analyze the stability: is this equilibrium stable against a small displacement *along* the line? *Perpendicular* to the line? (Call the point a saddle if it's stable one way and unstable the other.)

## Problem 2 — The Switch That Raises Resistance

A $12$ V battery drives $R_1=6\,\Omega$ in parallel with $R_2=3\,\Omega$; that combination is in series with $R_3=4\,\Omega$. A switch $S$ is connected **across** $R_2$.

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

Switch open: equivalent resistance, battery current, and the current through each resistor.

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

Switch closed (shorts $R_2$): new equivalent resistance and currents.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

Compare the battery's power output and the power in $R_2$ in the two cases. Explain what is surprising about the total resistance.

<details>

Initial stored energy.

<details>
<summary><b>3.2</b></summary>

## Problem 4 — The Velocity Selector

<details>
<summary><b>4.1</b></summary>

<details>

<details>
<summary><b>4.3</b></summary>

<details>
<summary><b>4.4</b></summary>

## Problem 5 — Pulling the Rod Through a Field

<details>
<summary><b>5.1</b></summary>

Find the terminal speed.

<details>
<summary><b>5.2</b></summary>

Show that the mechanical power supplied equals the electrical power dissipated in the resistor.

<details>
<summary><b>5.3</b></summary>

If $B$ is doubled, what happens to the terminal speed?

<details>
<summary><b>5.4</b></summary>

If the rod itself has resistance $r=0.50\,\Omega$ (in series with $R$), find the new terminal speed.
