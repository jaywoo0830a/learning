# E&M — Special: Electric Charge — Feel Builder

> **Purpose:** Five intuition-building problems on **electric charge** (quantization, conservation, charging) — tuned for someone who just finished mechanics.
> **Level:** Regular Physics / SAT Physics — **algebraic only, no calculus.**
> **How to use:** Solve the folded sub-problems in order. Full solutions (✅ Solution) live in [`solutions/1-solutions.md`](solutions/1-solutions.md) — open a problem's solution only after finishing it.
> **The core feel to build:** charge is a *property* that comes in tiny discrete lumps and is **never created or destroyed** — it only moves. Rubbing, touching, and grounding all just move electrons around.

## Problem 1 — The Electron Counter

<details>
<summary><b>1.1</b></summary>

A balloon is charged to $-3.2\times10^{-9}$ C. How many **excess electrons** does it carry?

<details>
<summary><b>1.2</b></summary>

A copper coin contains roughly $10^{22}$ atoms, each with one outer electron. Compare that enormous number with the excess-electron count from 1.1. What does this tell you about how much "net charge" everyday objects really carry?

<details>
<summary><b>1.3</b></summary>

Two identical metal spheres hold charges $+5e$ and $-3e$. They are touched together and then separated. Find the final charge on each, and verify that total charge is conserved.

## Problem 2 — The Charged Balloon and the Wall

<details>
<summary><b>2.1</b></summary>

You rub a balloon on your hair. Describe the charges on the balloon and your hair, and explain (using **polarization**) why the charged balloon then attracts small bits of neutral paper.

<details>
<summary><b>2.2</b></summary>

A positively charged rod is brought **near** (not touching) a neutral metal sphere. Describe how charge is distributed on the sphere.

<details>
<summary><b>2.3</b></summary>

While the rod is still near, the sphere is briefly **grounded** (touched with a wire to the ground), then the wire is removed, then the rod is moved away. What is the sphere's final net charge? This is **charging by induction**.

<details>

<details>
