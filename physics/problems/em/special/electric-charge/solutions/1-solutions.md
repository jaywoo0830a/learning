## Problem 1 — The Electron Counter

**1.1 —** $n=Q/e=(3.2\times10^{-9})/(1.6\times10^{-19})=2.0\times10^{10}$ **excess electrons**. The negative sign means electrons are in excess.

**1.2 —** The coin holds roughly $10^{22}$ electrons (and the same number of protons). The balloon's net charge is only $2\times10^{10}$ electrons — about **2 parts per trillion** of the total. Daily-life "charged" objects carry a minuscule imbalance in an enormous sea of charge; that's why charge is so easily transferred and so hard to notice.

**1.3 —** Total charge before $=+5e+(-3e)=+2e$. After touching, identical spheres share equally: **$+1e$ each**. Check: $+5e-3e=+1e+1e$ ✓ — nothing created or destroyed.

> **The feel:** charge comes in tiny fixed lumps ($e$), and the only thing that happens is these lumps moving around. Counting them is just arithmetic with $1.6\times10^{-19}$.

## Problem 2 — The Charged Balloon and the Wall

**2.1 —** Rubbing transfers electrons from your hair to the balloon: the **balloon becomes negative**, your hair positive. Neutral paper bits get **polarized** — their electrons shift slightly away from the balloon, leaving the near side slightly positive. Because the near side is closer, attraction wins, and the paper is pulled in.

**2.2 —** The $+$ rod attracts the sphere's electrons to the near side: **near side negative, far side positive**. The sphere as a whole is still neutral — charge just redistributed (this is *polarization* on a conductor).

**2.3 —** Grounding lets the **far-side positive charge** be neutralized by electrons from the ground. Remove the ground wire, then the rod: the sphere is left with a **net negative charge**. No contact with the rod was ever needed — that's charging by induction.

> **The feel:** conductors let charge move freely, insulators don't. Every charging trick (rubbing, induction, grounding) is just "move electrons around" — and you can attract neutral objects because charge can rearrange inside them.

## Problem 3 — The Conservation Ledger

**3.1 —** Losing 2 electrons means 2 fewer negatives: net charge $=+2e$.

**3.2 —** Before: $0$. After: $+e+(-e)=0$. **Charge is conserved** — a neutral particle decays into exactly balanced charges.

**3.3 —** Total $=+4.0+(-7.0)=-3.0\,\mu$C. Split equally: **$-1.5\,\mu$C each**. Check: $-3.0=-1.5-1.5$ ✓.

> **The feel:** charge, like energy and momentum in mechanics, is a **bookkeeping quantity that never changes in total**. Any process, from touching spheres to particle decay, must balance the ledger.

## Problem 4 — The "Big Charge" Misconception

**4.1 —** It **lost electrons**. In solids only electrons move; protons are locked in the nucleus. A positive charge is a *deficit* of electrons, not a "gain of positives."

**4.2 —** Rubbing doesn't create charge — it **transfers** it. The balloon's $-Q$ is exactly matched by the hair's $+Q$; total charge stays zero. "Creating" would violate conservation.

**4.3 —** They exchange electrons: one object loses $n$ electrons (becomes $+ne$), the other gains them (becomes $-ne$). Equal and opposite, and nothing was created — electrons just moved from one to the other.

> **The misconception fixed:** charging is never "making charge." It's always moving electrons from one place to another, so every charging event is a matched pair of $+$ and $-$.

## Problem 5 — The Touch and Share Puzzle

**5.1 —** Total $=+6-2=+4\,\mu$C. Identical spheres share equally: **$+2\,\mu$C each**.

**5.2 —** Not exactly half. Charge splits in proportion to each sphere's ability to hold charge (capacitance, which grows with radius): the **larger sphere ends up with more charge**. The equal split in 5.1 is special to identical spheres.

**5.3 —** The error ignores that charge **flows freely through the conductor on contact**. Electrons keep moving until both spheres reach the same potential — which, for identical spheres, means equal charge. The $-2$ sphere doesn't "keep" its negative charge; after contact both are $+2\,\mu$C, and the total $+4\,\mu$C is conserved.

> **The misconception fixed:** when conductors touch, they share until they match — they don't each keep their original sign. Always add the charges first, then share.
