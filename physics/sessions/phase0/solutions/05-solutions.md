## 🔥 Advanced Drills

**1.** To land exactly opposite, the boat's velocity relative to ground must be purely north. $\vec{v}_{\text{ground}} = \vec{v}_{\text{boat/water}} + \vec{v}_{\text{water}}$. Let $\vec{v}_{\text{boat/water}} = (v_x, v_y)$ with $v_x^2+v_y^2 = 25$. Need $v_x + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow v_x = -3$. Then $v_y = \sqrt{25-9} = 4$. Boat heads at angle $\sin^{-1}(3/5) \approx 36.9^\circ$ west of north. Crossing time: $200/4 = 50\text{ s}$.

**2.** $\vec{a} = d\vec{v}/dt = d(\vec{v}\,'+\vec{V})/dt = d\vec{v}\,'/dt + d\vec{V}/dt$. Since $\vec{V}$ is constant, $d\vec{V}/dt = 0$. Thus $\vec{a} = \vec{a}\,'$.

**3.** Ground: $\vec{r}(t) = \vec{V}_{\text{truck}}t + \vec{r}\,'(t)$ where $\vec{r}\,'$ is the projectile's position in the truck frame. Range in ground frame: $R = V_{\text{truck}}t_f + R'$. The truck moves forward during the flight, adding its displacement to the truck-frame range.

**4.** Ground frame: yes, acceleration is still $(0,-g)$ — gravity is the only force. Train frame: the train is accelerating, so it's **not** inertial. In the train frame, the ball appears to have an additional horizontal acceleration of $-2\text{ m/s}^2$ (backward) — a **fictitious force.** This is exactly why Kleppner Ch 9 is needed.

**5.** $\vec{v}_{12} = \vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2 = (2t, 3) - (2t+2, 2) = (-2, 1)$ — constant! $\vec{a}_{12} = \vec{a}_1 - \vec{a}_2 = (2,0) - (2,0) = (0,0)$ — zero relative acceleration. The two particles move with constant relative velocity.

## 🧠 Intuitional Drills

**1.** Lands back in your hand. In the train frame: pure vertical motion. In the ground frame: both ball and hand share the train's horizontal velocity — they stay aligned. This is the essence of Galilean relativity.

**2.** Longer. Headwind: ground speed = $150\text{ m/s}$, tailwind: $250\text{ m/s}$. $t = \frac{500000}{150} + \frac{500000}{250} = 3333 + 2000 = 5333\text{ s}$. No wind: $t = 2 \times 2500 = 5000\text{ s}$. The headwind hurts more than the tailwind helps because $1/150 + 1/250 > 2/200$.

**3.** $\vec{v}_{\text{ground}} = (20, 1)$, $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{401} \approx 20.02\text{ m/s}$. Adding perpendicular velocities barely changes the magnitude — the train's $20\text{ m/s}$ dominates.

**4.** $\vec{v}_{B\text{ rel }A} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = (0,20) - (20,0) = (-20, 20)$. Speed: $\sqrt{800} \approx 28.3\text{ m/s}$. Direction: $45^\circ$ — A sees B approaching from the $45^\circ$ direction (northeast if A is at origin).

**5.** $\vec{v}_{\text{east rel north}} = (15,0) - (0,20) = (15, -20)$. This points to the lower-right from the northbound driver's perspective. Angle east of south: $\tan^{-1}(15/20) \approx 37^\circ$.
