### A2
(a) Average = $(4.52 + 4.55 + 4.48) / 3 = 13.55 / 3 = 4.5166...$
4.52, 4.55, 4.48 each have 2 decimal places. Sum = 13.55 (2 decimal places).
Dividing by exact 3: answer gets 2 decimal places → **4.52 g** (rounded from 4.5166...).

(b) **Precise?** Yes. The measurements cluster tightly (range = 4.55 − 4.48 = 0.07 g, low spread).

(c) **Accurate?** Yes. The true value is 4.50 g. Average 4.52 g is close — the percent error is $((4.52-4.50)/4.50) \times 100\% = 0.44\%$.

(d) With 5.10 g added: new average = $(4.52 + 4.55 + 4.48 + 5.10) / 4 = 18.65 / 4 = 4.66$ g. Range widens to $5.10 - 4.48 = 0.62$ g. Precision drops. Accuracy also drops — average is now further from 4.50 g.

### A3
The student's approach is flawed. Here's the issue:

The expression is $(12.3 + 0.45) \times 2.0$.

**Correct approach:** Do each step with its own rule.
- Step 1 (addition): $12.3 + 0.45 = 12.75$. By the addition rule: 12.3 (1 decimal place, limiting). Keep $12.75$ unrounded on calculator.
- Step 2 (multiplication): $12.75 \times 2.0 = 25.5$. Now apply the multiplication rule: 2.0 has 2 sig figs (limiting). 25.5 → **26** (2 sig figs).

The student correctly identified the addition rule but then applied it to the final answer instead of just the intermediate. The final answer should be **26** (2 sig figs), not 25.5.

### A8
$$\frac{(14.25 - 3.0) \times 2.500}{(6.0 + 1.11)}$$

Step 1: $14.25 - 3.0 = 11.25$ (keep unrounded: 11.25).
14.25 (2 decimal places), 3.0 (1 decimal place, limiting). Intermediate: 11.3 (1 decimal place), but keep 11.25 on calculator.

6.0 (1 decimal place, limiting), 1.11 (2 decimal places). Intermediate: 7.1 (1 decimal place), but keep 7.11 on calculator.

Step 3: $11.25 \times 2.500 = 28.125$.

Step 4: $28.125 / 7.11 = 3.955...$

Now apply sig figs:
- Subtraction: 3.0 (2 sig figs), 14.25 (4 sig figs) → intermediate has 2 sig figs.
- Addition: 6.0 (2 sig figs), 1.11 (3 sig figs) → intermediate has... actually for +/−, use decimal places: 1 decimal place for 6.0.
- Then ×/÷: limiting is 2 sig figs (from 3.0 and 6.0).

**Answer: 4.0** (2 sig figs).

### A9
(a) Edge = 2.0 cm. Volume = $(2.0)^3 = 8.0$ cm³. (2 sig figs from 2.0 → actually $2.0^3$: the sig figs carry through the exponent: 2 sig figs → **8.0 cm³**.)

Wait — careful: $2.0^3 = 8.0$. The rule is: when raising to a power, the number of sig figs stays the same as the base. 2.0 has 2 sig figs → 8.0 has 2 sig figs.

(b) Edge = 2.00 cm. Volume = $(2.00)^3 = 8.00$ cm³. 2.00 has 3 sig figs → 8.00 has 3 sig figs.

(c) Percent difference = $|8.00 - 8.0| / 8.00 \times 100\% = 0.0/8.00$... Wait, $8.0$ with 2 sig figs could be anywhere from 7.95 to 8.05. $8.00$ with 3 sig figs could be 7.995 to 8.005.

The difference is in the third digit position: $8.00 - 8.0$ when treated as ranges: the values overlap. But as reported values, $8.0 = 8.0$ and $8.00 = 8.00$, so numerically: $(8.00 - 8.0)/8.00 = 0.00/8.00 = 0\%$ — but this is a sig fig illusion because 8.0 truncates the third digit.

(d) Volume = edge³. The cubing amplifies small differences. If the edge measurement differs by 0.05 cm (the uncertainty in 3.4 vs 3.40), then volume differs by roughly $3 \times (\text{edge})^2 \times 0.05 \approx 3 \times 11.6 \times 0.05 \approx 1.7$ cm³. This is why sig figs matter: a small uncertainty in length becomes a larger uncertainty in volume.

### A10
(a) Volume = 28.5 mL − 25.0 mL = 3.5 mL = 3.5 cm³.
Density = 15.74 g / 3.5 cm³ = 4.497... g/cm³.
3.5 has 2 sig figs (limiting). → **4.5 g/cm³** (2 sig figs).

(b) Volume of cube = edge³ → edge = $\sqrt[3]{V} = \sqrt[3]{3.5} = 1.518...$ cm.
2 sig figs → **1.5 cm**.

(c) Edge from ruler = 1.58 cm. Volume = $(1.58)^3 = 3.944$ cm³.
Density = 15.74 g / 3.944 cm³ = 3.991... g/cm³.
1.58 (3 sig figs), 15.74 (4 sig figs) → limiting 3 sig figs → **3.99 g/cm³**.

(d) Water displacement gave 4.5 g/cm³ (2 sig figs). Ruler gave 3.99 g/cm³ (3 sig figs).
- **More precise:** Ruler method (3 sig figs vs. 2).
- **More accurate:** Water displacement is generally more accurate because it directly measures volume without assuming perfect cube shape. But with only 2 sig figs, the precision is lower.
- This illustrates the precision-vs-accuracy tradeoff: ruler has more sig figs but assumes perfect geometry; displacement has fewer sig figs but measures actual volume directly.
