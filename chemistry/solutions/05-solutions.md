## Basic Drill Solutions (B1–B10)

### B1

Balance: $2Na + Cl_2 \to 2NaCl$.

From the equation: 2 mol Na → 2 mol NaCl, ratio = 2:2 = 1:1.
mol NaCl = 3.00 mol Na × (2 mol NaCl / 2 mol Na) = 3.00 mol NaCl.

$$\boxed{2Na + Cl_2 \to 2NaCl;\; 3.00 \text{ mol NaCl}}$$

---

### B2

Balance: $CH_4 + 2O_2 \to CO_2 + 2H_2O$.

From equation: 1 mol $CH_4$ needs 2 mol $O_2$.
mol $O_2$ = 2.00 mol $CH_4$ × (2 mol $O_2$ / 1 mol $CH_4$) = 4.00 mol $O_2$.

$$\boxed{CH_4 + 2O_2 \to CO_2 + 2H_2O;\; 4.00 \text{ mol } O_2}$$

---

### B3

Balance: $2Mg + O_2 \to 2MgO$.

Molar mass Mg = 24.31 g/mol.
mol Mg = 12.0 g ÷ 24.31 g/mol = 0.49362 mol.

Mole ratio: 2 Mg : 2 MgO = 1:1.
mol MgO = 0.49362 mol.

Molar mass MgO = 24.31 + 16.00 = 40.31 g/mol.
g MgO = 0.49362 mol × 40.31 g/mol = 19.90 g.

$$\boxed{2Mg + O_2 \to 2MgO;\; 19.9 \text{ g } MgO}\quad\text{(3 sig figs, limited by 12.0)}$$

### B4

Balance: $2KClO_3 \to 2KCl + 3O_2$.

Molar mass $KClO_3$ = 39.10 + 35.45 + 3(16.00) = 122.55 g/mol.
mol $KClO_3$ = 50.0 g ÷ 122.55 g/mol = 0.40799 mol.

Mole ratio: 2 $KClO_3$ : 3 $O_2$ = 2:3.
mol $O_2$ = 0.40799 mol × (3/2) = 0.61199 mol.

Molar mass $O_2$ = 32.00 g/mol.
g $O_2$ = 0.61199 mol × 32.00 g/mol = 19.58 g.

$$\boxed{2KClO_3 \to 2KCl + 3O_2;\; 19.6 \text{ g } O_2}\quad\text{(3 sig figs, limited by 50.0)}$$

### B9

Balance: $N_2 + 3H_2 \to 2NH_3$.

**Part 1:**
Molar mass $N_2$ = 2(14.01) = 28.02 g/mol.
mol $N_2$ = 28.0 g ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 0.99929 mol.

Mole ratio: 1 $N_2$ : 2 $NH_3$.
mol $NH_3$ = 0.99929 mol × 2 = 1.9986 mol.

Molar mass $NH_3$ = 14.01 + 3(1.008) = 17.034 g/mol.
g $NH_3$ = 1.9986 mol × 17.034 g/mol = 34.04 g.

$$\boxed{\text{Part 1: } N_2 + 3H_2 \to 2NH_3;\; 34.0 \text{ g } NH_3}\quad\text{(3 sig figs)}$$

**Part 2:**
mol $H_2$ consumed = 0.99929 mol $N_2$ × (3/1) = 2.9979 mol $H_2$.
g $H_2$ = 2.9979 mol × 2.016 g/mol = 6.044 g.

$$\boxed{\text{Part 2: } 6.04 \text{ g } H_2 \text{ consumed}}\quad\text{(3 sig figs)}$$

### A3

The student made two critical mistakes:

**Mistake 1:** Used only the mass of carbon (12.01) instead of the full molar mass of $CO_2$ (44.01 g/mol) in the final step. The question asks for grams of $CO_2$, not grams of C within the $CO_2$. You must multiply moles of $CO_2$ by the molar mass of $CO_2$, not just the carbon portion.

**Mistake 2:** Implicitly assumed that "mol $CO_2$ = mol C" means "g $CO_2$ = g C." While 1 mol $CO_2$ contains 1 mol C, the masses are different: 1 mol $CO_2$ weighs 44.01 g, and 1 mol C weighs 12.01 g — a factor of ~3.66 difference.

Correct calculation:
mol $C_3H_8$ = 50.0 g ÷ 44.094 g/mol = 1.1339 mol.
mol $CO_2$ = 1.1339 mol × 3 = 3.4017 mol.
g $CO_2$ = 3.4017 mol × 44.01 g/mol = 149.7 g → **150. g $CO_2$**.

$$\boxed{\text{The student used the mass of C (12.01) instead of } CO_2 \text{ (44.01) in the grams step. Correct: 150. g } CO_2.}$$

### A5

Balanced: $N_2 + 3H_2 \to 2NH_3$.

(a) Molar mass $N_2 = 2 \times 14.01 = 28.02$ g/mol.
mol $N_2 = 50.0 \div 28.02 = 1.7844$ mol.

$$\boxed{10.8 \text{ g } H_2 \text{ consumed}}$$

(c) **Conservation check:**
Reactants: $50.0 \text{ g } N_2 + 10.79 \text{ g } H_2 = 60.79$ g.

### A6

(a) Combustion: $C_2H_5OH + O_2 \to CO_2 + H_2O$.
Balance C: $C_2H_5OH + O_2 \to 2CO_2 + H_2O$.
Balance H: $C_2H_5OH + O_2 \to 2CO_2 + 3H_2O$.
Balance O: Right: $2\times2 + 3\times1 = 7$ O. Left: $1$ (from ethanol) + $2$ (per $O_2$) = need $6$ more → $3$ $O_2$.

$$C_2H_5OH + 3O_2 \to 2CO_2 + 3H_2O$$

(b) Molar mass $C_2H_5OH = 2(12.01) + 6(1.008) + 16.00 = 46.068$ g/mol.
mol ethanol = $25.0 \div 46.068 = 0.54268$ mol.

$CO_2$: mol = $0.54268 \times 2 = 1.0854$ mol. g = $1.0854 \times 44.01 = 47.77$ g.
$H_2O$: mol = $0.54268 \times 3 = 1.6280$ mol. g = $1.6280 \times 18.016 = 29.33$ g.

$$\boxed{47.8 \text{ g } CO_2 \text{ and } 29.3 \text{ g } H_2O}\quad\text{(3 sig figs)}$$

(c) **The $O_2$ coefficient trap:**
A student who uses $O_2$ coefficient (3) instead of product coefficients:
- For $CO_2$: mol $CO_2 = 0.54268 \times 3 = 1.6280$ mol (wrong — should be ×2).
g $CO_2 = 1.6280 \times 44.01 = 71.65$ g (off by 50%).
- For $H_2O$: mol $H_2O = 0.54268 \times 3 = 1.6280$ mol (coincidentally same as correct for $H_2O$ since its coefficient IS 3).

The trap: the $O_2$ coefficient (3) happens to equal the $H_2O$ coefficient (3) in this case, so $H_2O$ comes out correct but $CO_2$ (coefficient 2) does not. Never assume — use the product's OWN coefficient.
