### A4

Yes, the student's calculation is **correct**.

$45.0 \text{ m/s} \times \dfrac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}} \times \dfrac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}} = 45.0 \times \dfrac{3600}{1000} = 45.0 \times 3.6 = 162 \text{ km/h}$

**Why 3600 goes on top:** We want km/h (km on top, h on bottom). The starting unit is m/s (m on top, s on bottom).
- m → km: need $\dfrac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}}$ — km on top ✓
- s → h: need $\dfrac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}}$ — wait, we want to CANCEL seconds. s is on the bottom of the starting unit. So we put s on top of the conversion: $\dfrac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}}$ — the s cancels and h ends on bottom. ✓

To check: $45.0 \times (1/1000) \times 3600 = 162$ ✓

$$\boxed{162 \text{ km/h} \text{ — correct!}}$$
