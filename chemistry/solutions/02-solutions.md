## Basic Drill Solutions (B1–B10)

### B1
$5.00 \text{ km} \times \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ km}} = 5.00 \times 10^3 \text{ m}$
**Answer: 5.00 × 10³ m** (3 sig figs).

**Answer: 0.450 g** (3 sig figs).

### B3
$2.50 \text{ h} \times \frac{60 \text{ min}}{1 \text{ h}} \times \frac{60 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ min}} = 2.50 \times 60 \times 60 = 9000 \text{ s}$
**Answer: 9.00 × 10³ s** (3 sig figs; the 60s are exact).

### B4
$65.0 \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1.609 \text{ km}}{1 \text{ mi}} = 104.585 \text{ km/h}$
65.0 (3 sig figs), 1.609 (4 sig figs) → limiting 3 sig figs.
**Answer: 105 km/h** (3 sig figs).

---

### B5
$2.00 \text{ L} \times \frac{1000 \text{ mL}}{1 \text{ L}} = 2.00 \times 10^3 \text{ mL}$
$2.00 \times 10^3 \text{ mL} = 2.00 \times 10^3 \text{ cm}^3$ (since 1 mL = 1 cm³ exactly).
**Answer: 2.00 × 10³ mL = 2.00 × 10³ cm³** (3 sig figs).

---

### B6
$0.850 \frac{\text{g}}{\text{mL}} \times \frac{1 \text{ kg}}{1000 \text{ g}} \times \frac{1000 \text{ mL}}{1 \text{ L}}$

The 1000s cancel: $\frac{1}{1000} \times \frac{1000}{1} = 1$.

So: $0.850 \frac{\text{g}}{\text{mL}} = 0.850 \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}}$.

**Answer: 0.850 kg/L** (3 sig figs). Same number — because the conversions cancel!

### B7
Mass = $0.450 \text{ cm}^3 \times \frac{19.3 \text{ g}}{1 \text{ cm}^3} = 8.685 \text{ g}$.
0.450 (3 sig figs), 19.3 (3 sig figs) → **8.69 g** (3 sig figs).

In kg: $8.69 \text{ g} \times \frac{1 \text{ kg}}{1000 \text{ g}} = 0.00869 \text{ kg} = 8.69 \times 10^{-3} \text{ kg}$.
**Answer: 8.69 g = 8.69 × 10⁻³ kg** (3 sig figs).

### B8
$50.0 \text{ m}^3 \times \frac{1000 \text{ L}}{1 \text{ m}^3} = 5.00 \times 10^4 \text{ L}$
**Answer: 5.00 × 10⁴ L** (3 sig figs).

### B9

100.0 (4 sig figs) → **91.44 m** (4 sig figs).

Part 2: $91.44 \text{ m} \times \frac{100 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ m}} = 9144 \text{ cm}$.
**Answer: 91.44 m = 9.144 × 10³ cm** (4 sig figs).

### B10
$3.00 \times 10^8 \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \times \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}} \times \frac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}}$

= $3.00 \times 10^8 \times 3600 / 1000$
= $3.00 \times 10^8 \times 3.6$
= $1.08 \times 10^9 \text{ km/h}$

**Answer: 1.08 × 10⁹ km/h** (3 sig figs).

### A3

### A4
The student's setup: $45.0 \times (1 \text{ km}/1000 \text{ m}) \times (3600 \text{ s}/1 \text{ h}) = 162 \text{ km/h}$.

**Yes, this is correct.** Reason:
- Start: 45.0 m/s = $\frac{45.0 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ s}}$
- Convert m → km: $\frac{45.0 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ s}} \times \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}}$ → m cancels, km on top.
- Convert s → h: $\frac{45.0 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ s}} \times \frac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}}$ → s cancels, h on bottom.
- Final: $\frac{45.0 \times 3600}{1000} = 162 \text{ km/h}$.

The 3600 goes on top because we have "per second" and want "per hour" — there are 3600 seconds in an hour, so one second is a smaller unit. To convert a per-second rate to a per-hour rate, multiply by 3600.

**Answer: 162 km/h** (3 sig figs). Correct.

### A5
*Learner-generated problem — example solution:*

Problem: "A runner completes a marathon (26.2 miles) in 3.50 hours. Find the runner's average speed in meters per second. (1 mi = 1.609 km, 1 km = 1000 m, 1 h = 3600 s.)"

Solution:
$26.2 \text{ mi} \times \frac{1.609 \text{ km}}{1 \text{ mi}} \times \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ km}} = 42,155.8 \text{ m}$

$3.50 \text{ h} \times \frac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}} = 12,600 \text{ s}$

Speed = $42,155.8 \text{ m} / 12,600 \text{ s} = 3.345... \text{ m/s}$.
26.2 (3 sig figs), 3.50 (3 sig figs) → **3.35 m/s** (3 sig figs).

### A6
*Learner-generated problem — example solution:*

Problem: "Convert 5.00 m³ to cm³."

Wrong way: $5.00 \text{ m}^3 \times \frac{100 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ m}} = 500 \text{ cm}^3$. Off by factor of 1,000,000.

Right way: $5.00 \text{ m}^3 \times \left(\frac{100 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ m}}\right)^3 = 5.00 \times (100)^3 = 5.00 \times 1,000,000 = 5.00 \times 10^6 \text{ cm}^3$.

The wrong answer (500) is 10,000× too small. The right answer is $5.00 \times 10^6$ cm³.

### A8
Volume = $\pi r^2 h = \pi (1.50 \text{ m})^2 (4.00 \text{ m}) = \pi \times 2.25 \times 4.00 = 28.274... \text{ m}^3$.

Convert to cm³ (and mL): $28.274 \text{ m}^3 \times (10^6 \text{ cm}^3 / 1 \text{ m}^3) = 2.8274 \times 10^7 \text{ cm}^3 = 2.8274 \times 10^7 \text{ mL}$.

In metric tons: $2.4881 \times 10^4 \text{ kg} / 1000 = 24.88 \text{ metric tons}$.

Sig figs: 1.50 (3), 4.00 (3), 0.880 (3) → **24.9 metric tons** (3 sig figs).

### A10
(a) Partner assumes 150. lb is the mass. In kg:
$150. \text{ lb} \times \frac{1 \text{ kg}}{2.205 \text{ lb}} = 68.027... \text{ kg}$.
The partner actually used **68.0 kg** (3 sig figs, from 150.).

(b) Fuel ratio: 0.200 kg fuel per 1 kg satellite mass.
Fuel loaded = $68.0 \text{ kg satellite} \times 0.200 = 13.6 \text{ kg fuel}$.
**Answer: 13.6 kg** (3 sig figs, from 0.200).

(c) Is it enough? The satellite actually has mass 150. kg. Fuel needed = $150. \text{ kg} \times 0.200 = 30.0 \text{ kg}$.
Loaded: 13.6 kg. Deficit: $30.0 - 13.6 = 16.4 \text{ kg}$.
**No, it's not enough.** The satellite is short by 16.4 kg of fuel — NASA's unit mix-up nearly doomed the mission.

(This is based on the real Mars Climate Orbiter disaster — unit confusion between pound-force and newtons caused the loss of a \$327M spacecraft.)
