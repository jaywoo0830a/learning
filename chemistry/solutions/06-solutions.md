## Basic Drill Solutions (B1–B10)

### B1

$N_2 + 3H_2 \to 2NH_3$. 14.0 g $N_2$ + 3.00 g $H_2$.

mol $N_2 = 14.0 \div 28.02 = 0.49964$ mol.
mol $H_2 = 3.00 \div 2.016 = 1.4881$ mol.

Divide by coefficients: $N_2 = 0.49964/1 = 0.4996$, $H_2 = 1.4881/3 = 0.4960$.

$0.4960 < 0.4996 \;\to\;$ **$H_2$ is limiting.**

Theoretical yield from $H_2$: mol $NH_3 = 1.4881 \times (2/3) = 0.99207$ mol.
g $NH_3 = 0.99207 \times 17.034 = 16.90$ g.

$$\boxed{\text{(a) } H_2 \text{ limiting} \qquad \text{(b) } 16.9 \text{ g } NH_3}\quad\text{(3 sig figs)}$$

### A5

$2H_2 + O_2 \to 2H_2O$. 10.0 g $H_2$ and 10.0 g $O_2$.

(a) Molar mass $H_2 = 2.016$ g/mol, $O_2 = 32.00$ g/mol.
mol $H_2 = 10.0 \div 2.016 = 4.9603$ mol.
mol $O_2 = 10.0 \div 32.00 = 0.31250$ mol.

(b) Divide by coefficients:
$H_2$ ratio = $4.9603 / 2 = 2.4802$
$O_2$ ratio = $0.31250 / 1 = 0.31250$
$0.31250 < 2.4802$ → **$O_2$ is limiting.**

(c) From $O_2$: mol $H_2O = 0.31250 \times 2 = 0.62500$ mol.
g $H_2O = 0.62500 \times 18.016 = 11.260$ g.

$$\boxed{11.3 \text{ g } H_2O}\quad\text{(3 sig figs)}$$

(d) **Why mass alone doesn't determine the LR:**
10.0 g of $H_2$ is $4.96$ mol, but 10.0 g of $O_2$ is only $0.313$ mol. $O_2$ has a much higher molar mass (32.00 vs 2.016 g/mol), so the same mass gives far fewer moles. The LR depends on **moles relative to coefficients**, not grams. Equal masses never tell you the LR — you must always convert to moles first.

### A9

mol NaCl = $x/58.44$, produces mol AgCl = $x/58.44$.
g AgCl = $(x/58.44) \times 143.32 = 2.4525x$.

mol NaBr = $y/102.89$, produces mol AgBr = $y/102.89$.

Total precipitate: $2.4525x + 1.8249y = 10.38$.

System: $x + y = 5.00$, $2.4525x + 1.8249y = 10.38$.

Substitute $y = 5.00 - x$:
$2.4525x + 1.8249(5.00 - x) = 10.38$
$2.4525x + 9.1245 - 1.8249x = 10.38$
$0.6276x = 1.2555$
$x = 2.000$ g NaCl.

Mass $\%$ NaCl = $2.000/5.00 \times 100\% = 40.00\%$.

$$\boxed{40.0\% \text{ NaCl by mass}}$$
