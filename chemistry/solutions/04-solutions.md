### B10

**$H_2$ path:**

Molar mass $H_2$ = 2 × 1.008 = 2.016 g/mol.
mol $H_2$ = 5.00 g ÷ 2.016 g/mol = 2.4802 mol.
molecules $H_2$ = 2.4802 mol × (6.022 × 10²³) = 1.494 × 10²⁴ molecules.

**$N_2$ path:**

Molar mass $N_2$ = 2 × 14.01 = 28.02 g/mol.
mol $N_2$ = 5.00 g ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 0.17844 mol.
molecules $N_2$ = 0.17844 mol × (6.022 × 10²³) = 1.075 × 10²³ molecules.

$$\boxed{5.00 \text{ g of } H_2 \text{ contains } 1.49 \times 10^{24} \text{ molecules, } N_2 \text{ has } 1.07 \times 10^{23}}$$

$\therefore$ $H_2$ has about **14× more molecules** than $N_2$ for the same mass.

### A3

The student made a contradiction: they said "molar mass of nitrogen gas is 14.01 g/mol" but nitrogen gas is $N_2$, so its molar mass is 2 × 14.01 = 28.02 g/mol. Then 28.02 g of $N_2$ ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 1.00 mol, not 2.00 mol.

If the student meant "molar mass of N atom = 14.01 g/mol, so 28.02 g of $N_2$ = 28.02/28.02 = 1.00 mol $N_2$ = 2.00 mol N atoms", that would be correct — but you must distinguish between mol of molecules and mol of atoms.

$$\boxed{\text{The molar mass of } N_2 \text{ is 28.02 g/mol, not 14.01. } 28.02 \text{ g } N_2 = 1.00 \text{ mol } N_2 \text{ molecules} = 2.00 \text{ mol N atoms.}}$$

### A5

**Example problem:** "A weather balloon contains 200.0 g of helium gas. How many helium atoms are in the balloon?"

Solution:
Molar mass He = 4.003 g/mol (monatomic — He is NOT diatomic; it's a noble gas, exists as single atoms).
mol He = 200.0 g ÷ 4.003 g/mol = 49.963 mol.
atoms He = 49.963 mol × (6.022 × 10²³ atoms/mol) = 3.009 × 10²⁵ atoms.

$$\boxed{3.009 \times 10^{25} \text{ He atoms}}\quad\text{(4 sig figs)}$$

Trap note: Helium is monatomic (He, not $He_2$). If a student mistakenly used $He_2$ (molar mass = 8.006 g/mol), they'd get half the correct number of atoms.

### A6

**Example problem:** "A sample of methane ($CH_4$) contains exactly $3.011 \times 10^{23}$ molecules. What is the mass of the sample in grams?"

Solution:
$3.011 \times 10^{23}$ is exactly half of Avogadro's number → 0.5000 mol $CH_4$.
Molar mass $CH_4$ = 12.01 + 4(1.008) = 16.042 g/mol.
mass = 0.5000 mol × 16.042 g/mol = 8.021 g.

$$\boxed{8.021 \text{ g } CH_4}\quad\text{(4 sig figs)}$$

Significance: $3.011 \times 10^{23} = N_A/2$, so this is exactly half a mole — a favorite trick on multiple-choice exams.

### A10

Molar mass hemoglobin ≈ $6.5 \times 10^4$ g/mol.

(a) Molar mass Fe = 55.85 g/mol.
mol Fe = 0.00340 g ÷ 55.85 g/mol = $6.0877 \times 10^{-5}$ mol.

(b) mol hemoglobin = 5.000 g ÷ ($6.5 \times 10^4$ g/mol) = $7.6923 \times 10^{-5}$ mol.

(c) Ratio: $(6.0877 \times 10^{-5}) / (7.6923 \times 10^{-5}) = 0.7914 \approx 0.8$ Fe atoms per hemoglobin molecule (using the approximate molar mass — which is rough).

(d) Using exactly 4 Fe/molecule: mol hemoglobin = $6.0877 \times 10^{-5} / 4 = 1.5219 \times 10^{-5}$ mol.
More precise molar mass = 5.000 g / ($1.5219 \times 10^{-5}$ mol) = $3.285 \times 10^5$ g/mol.

$$\boxed{\text{(a) } 6.09 \times 10^{-5} \text{ mol Fe} \quad \text{(b) } 7.69 \times 10^{-5} \text{ mol hemoglobin} \quad \text{(c) } \approx 0.8 \text{ Fe/molecule} \quad \text{(d) } 3.29 \times 10^5 \text{ g/mol}}$$
