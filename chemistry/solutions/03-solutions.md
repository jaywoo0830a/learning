### A4

The partner is **correct**. The empirical formula is $CH_2O$.

The student's mistake: they took the raw mole values (C ≈ 3.33, H ≈ 6.66, O ≈ 3.33) and multiplied by 3 without dividing by the smallest first. By coincidence, multiplying by 3 gave $C_3H_6O_3$, which IS a possible molecular formula — but it's NOT the simplest ratio.

The correct procedure:
1. Divide all by smallest (3.33) → C: 1, H: 2, O: 1 → $CH_2O$ (empirical)
2. Only then, multiply by $n$ if finding the molecular formula.

$$\boxed{CH_2O \text{ is correct (empirical)}}$$

### A5

**Problem:** A compound is 39.34% Na and 60.66% Cl by mass. Its molar mass is 58.44 g/mol. (a) Find empirical formula. (b) Find molecular formula. (c) Are they the same? Why?

**Solution:**
(a) Assume 100.0 g: Na = 39.34 g, Cl = 60.66 g.
mol Na = $39.34/22.99 = 1.711$ mol
mol Cl = $60.66/35.45 = 1.711$ mol
Ratio = 1 : 1 → **NaCl**

(b) Empirical formula mass = $22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44$ g/mol.
$n = 58.44 / 58.44 = 1.00$
Molecular formula = NaCl × 1 = **NaCl**

(c) **They are the same** because the molar mass ($58.44$ g/mol) equals the empirical formula mass. This happens when $n = 1$.

(d) The compound is **sodium chloride** (table salt).

$$\boxed{NaCl \text{ — empirical = molecular because } n = 1}$$

### A6

**Problem:** A compound is 72.36% Fe and 27.64% O by mass.

(a) Assume 100.0 g: Fe = 72.36 g, O = 27.64 g.
mol Fe = $72.36/55.85 = 1.296$ mol
mol O = $27.64/16.00 = 1.728$ mol

(b) Divide by smaller (1.296):
Fe: $1.296/1.296 = 1.00$
O: $1.728/1.296 = 1.333...$

(c) Ratio = 1 : 1.333... = 1 : $4/3$
Multiply by 3: (1 × 3) : ($4/3$ × 3) = 3 : 4

(d) Empirical formula = **$Fe_3O_4$** (magnetite / iron(II,III) oxide).

**Why multiply by 3?** The .33 ending means the true ratio is $1 : 1\frac{1}{3} = 3 : 4$. Multiplying all numbers by 3 converts the fractional ratio to whole numbers. Rounding 1.33 to 1 would give $FeO$, which is a different compound entirely!

$$\boxed{Fe_3O_4 \text{ (magnetite)}}$$
