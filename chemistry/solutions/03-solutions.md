### B1

### B3
Density = $45.0 \text{ g} / 5.00 \text{ cm}^3 = 9.00 \text{ g/cm}^3$.
9.00 > 1.00 (water) → sinks in water.
9.00 < 13.6 (mercury) → floats in mercury.
**Answer: 9.00 g/cm³. Sinks in water, floats in mercury.** Reason: its density (9.00) is between the two liquid densities (1.00 and 13.6).

### A5
*Learner-generated problem — example solution:*

Problem: "A compound is 52.1% C, 13.1% H, 34.7% O by mass. Its molar mass is 46.1 g/mol. Find both the empirical and molecular formulas."

Assume 100.0 g:
mol C = $52.1 / 12.01 = 4.338$ mol.
mol H = $13.1 / 1.008 = 12.996$ mol.
mol O = $34.7 / 16.00 = 2.169$ mol.

Divide by 2.169:
C: $4.338 / 2.169 = 2.00$
H: $12.996 / 2.169 = 5.99 \approx 6$
O: $2.169 / 2.169 = 1.00$

Empirical formula: C₂H₆O. Empirical mass = $2(12.01) + 6(1.008) + 16.00 = 46.07$ g/mol.
$n = 46.1 / 46.07 = 1.00$.

**Empirical = Molecular = C₂H₆O** (they are the same because the molar mass equals the empirical formula mass).

### A6
*Learner-generated problem — example solution:*

Problem: "A compound contains 72.4% iron and 27.6% oxygen by mass. Find the empirical formula."

Assume 100.0 g:
mol Fe = $72.4 / 55.85 = 1.296$ mol.
mol O = $27.6 / 16.00 = 1.725$ mol.

Divide by 1.296:
Fe: $1.296 / 1.296 = 1.00$
O: $1.725 / 1.296 = 1.33$ ← This is 4/3, not an integer.

Multiply ALL by 3:
Fe: $1.00 \times 3 = 3$
O: $1.33 \times 3 = 4$

**Empirical formula: Fe₃O₄** (magnetite).

Without multiplying by 3, you'd get Fe₁O₁.₃₃, which is chemically meaningless. Multiplying by 3 converts the fractional subscript to a whole number.

### A8
(a) All C in original → all C in CO₂.
Mass of C = mass of CO₂ × (molar mass C / molar mass CO₂)
= $1.466 \text{ g} \times (12.01 / 44.01) = 1.466 \times 0.27289 = 0.4001 \text{ g C}$.
**0.400 g C** (3 sig figs, from 1.466 with 4 sig figs — but answer from calculation: let's use 3).

(b) All H in original → all H in H₂O.
Mass of H = mass of H₂O × (2 × molar mass H / molar mass H₂O)
= $0.600 \text{ g} \times (2.016 / 18.016) = 0.600 \times 0.11193 = 0.06716 \text{ g H}$.
**0.0672 g H** (3 sig figs).

(c) Mass of O = total mass − mass C − mass H
= $1.000 \text{ g} - 0.4001 \text{ g} - 0.06716 \text{ g} = 0.53274 \text{ g O}$.
**0.533 g O** (3 sig figs).

Check: $0.400 + 0.0672 + 0.533 = 1.0002 \approx 1.000$. ✓

(d) Convert to moles:
mol C = $0.4001 / 12.01 = 0.03331$ mol.
mol H = $0.06716 / 1.008 = 0.06663$ mol.
mol O = $0.5327 / 16.00 = 0.03329$ mol.

Divide by smallest (0.03329):
C: $0.03331 / 0.03329 = 1.00$

### A9
(a) Mass of anhydrous $MgSO_4$ = $5.00 \text{ g} - 2.44 \text{ g} = 2.56 \text{ g}$.
**2.56 g MgSO₄** (3 sig figs).

(b) Molar mass of $MgSO_4$ = $24.31 + 32.07 + 4(16.00) = 120.38$ g/mol.
mol $MgSO_4$ = $2.56 / 120.38 = 0.02127$ mol.

Molar mass of $H_2O$ = $18.016$ g/mol.
mol $H_2O$ = $2.44 / 18.016 = 0.1354$ mol.

(c) $x = \text{mol }H_2O / \text{mol }MgSO_4 = 0.1354 / 0.02127 = 6.37$.
This is close to 7 — but 6.37 suggests $x = 7$ (epsom salt is $MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$). The discrepancy is within experimental error.

Rounding: $0.1354 / 0.02127 = 6.37 \approx 6$ if strict rounding, but 7 is the known value.
→ **$x \approx 7$** (experimentally, $x = 6.4$; the known hydrate is $x = 7$).

(d) **Formula: MgSO₄ · 7H₂O** (magnesium sulfate heptahydrate, Epsom salt).
