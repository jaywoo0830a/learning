# Chemistry Curriculum v1: The Calculator's Path — Honors Chemistry

> **Ultimate Goal:** Internalize every quantitative pattern in Honors Chemistry so thoroughly that calculations become automatic — and walk into any Honors Chemistry exam fully prepared.
> **Method:** **Problems first, concepts later.** Every session opens with a calculation you cannot yet do — by the end, you can do 20 of them.
> **Concept Reference:** "Everything You Need to Ace Chemistry in One Big Fat Notebook" (Big Fat Notebooks). Read the relevant chapter **after** completing the session.
> **Principle:** The calculator is your laboratory. Numbers reveal patterns; patterns become procedures; procedures become instinct.
> **Drill Structure:** Every session = 5~8 worked examples + **10 Basic Drills** + **10 Advanced Drills** = exam-level fluency.
> **Scale:** 20 sessions, approximately 50 hours. 400 total drill problems across the curriculum.
> **Duration:** 120–180 minutes per session (50% worked examples, 50% drills).

## Phase 3: States & Solutions — Gases, Liquids, Concentrations (4 sessions, ~6h)

> **Core Question:** "How do temperature, pressure, and concentration change what a substance does?"

| # | Topic | Opening Problem | Calculation Skills Built | Big Fat Notebook | Time |
|:--:|------|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|
| 09 | **Gas Laws: Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, Combined** | "A balloon at 25°C and 1.00 atm has volume 2.50 L. You take it outside where it's −5.0°C and the pressure is 0.920 atm. What's its new volume? Will it shrink or expand?" | ① Boyle: $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ (constant $T, n$) ② Charles: $V_1/T_1 = V_2/T_2$ (constant $P, n$) ③ Gay-Lussac: $P_1/T_1 = P_2/T_2$ (constant $V, n$) ④ Combined: $P_1V_1/T_1 = P_2V_2/T_2$ ⑤ **Temperature MUST be in Kelvin** — the #1 trap | Ch 11 | 150 min |
| 10 | **Ideal Gas Law & Dalton's Partial Pressure** | "A 5.00 L tank at 27.0°C contains 16.0 g of $O_2$ and 14.0 g of $N_2$. What is the total pressure? What is the partial pressure of each gas?" | ① $PV = nRT$: solving for any variable ② $R$ selection: 0.08206 L·atm/mol·K ③ Molar mass from gas density: $M = dRT/P$ ④ Dalton: $P_\text{total} = P_A + P_B + \cdots$ ⑤ Mole fraction: $X_A = n_A/n_\text{total}$, $P_A = X_A \cdot P_\text{total}$ ⑥ Gas collected over water: subtract vapor pressure | Ch 11 | 150 min |
| 11 | **Concentration Units & Colligative Properties** | "You dissolve 34.2 g of sucrose ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$) in 250.0 g of water. What is the boiling point of this solution? ($K_b = 0.512$ °C/m for water)" | ① Mass percent, mole fraction, molality ($m$) ② Converting between all concentration units ③ $\Delta T_b = i K_b m$, $\Delta T_f = i K_f m$ ④ van't Hoff factor $i$: electrolyte dissociation ⑤ Osmotic pressure: $\Pi = iMRT$ | Ch 15 | 150 min |
| 12 | **Gas Stoichiometry & STP** | "Sodium azide ($NaN_3$) decomposes to inflate airbags: $2NaN_3 \to 2Na + 3N_2$. How many grams of $NaN_3$ are needed to produce 60.0 L of $N_2$ at 25.0°C and 1.10 atm?" | ① Gas stoichiometry: g → mol → mol gas → $V$ via $PV=nRT$ ② STP conditions (0°C, 1 atm): 1 mol = 22.4 L ③ Non-STP: always use $PV=nRT$ ④ Combined stoichiometry: solid + gas in one reaction ⑤ Gas density at non-STP conditions | Ch 11 | 150 min |

## Phase 4: Reactions & Energy — Thermochemistry, Equilibrium, Acids/Bases (5 sessions, ~8h)

> **Core Question:** "How much heat? How far does it go? How acidic?"

| # | Topic | Opening Problem | Calculation Skills Built | Big Fat Notebook | Time |
|:--:|------|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|
| 13 | **Calorimetry & Enthalpy** | "You burn 1.50 g of ethanol ($C_2H_5OH$) in a bomb calorimeter with heat capacity 8.70 kJ/°C. The temperature rises from 22.00°C to 30.50°C. What is $\Delta H_\text{combustion}$ of ethanol in kJ/mol?" | ① $q = mc\Delta T$ and $q = C_\text{cal}\Delta T$ ② Coffee-cup vs. bomb calorimetry ③ Enthalpy from calorimetry data ④ $\Delta H$ per mole from $\Delta H$ per gram ⑤ Phase change: $q = n\Delta H_\text{fus/vap}$ (heating curves) | Ch 13 | 150 min |
| 14 | **Hess's Law & Standard Enthalpy of Formation** | "Calculate $\Delta H$ for $2C(s) + H_2(g) \to C_2H_2(g)$ using: (1) $C_2H_2 + \frac{5}{2}O_2 \to 2CO_2 + H_2O$, $\Delta H = -1299$ kJ (2) $C + O_2 \to CO_2$, $\Delta H = -393.5$ kJ (3) $H_2 + \frac{1}{2}O_2 \to H_2O$, $\Delta H = -285.8$ kJ" | ① Hess's Law: flipping, multiplying, adding equations ② $\Delta H^\circ_f$ table: $\Delta H^\circ_\text{rxn} = \sum n\Delta H^\circ_f(\text{products}) - \sum n\Delta H^\circ_f(\text{reactants})$ ③ Bond energy method: $\Delta H = \sum$ bonds broken − $\sum$ bonds formed ④ Deciding which method is faster for a given problem | Ch 13 | 150 min |
| 15 | **Equilibrium & $K_{eq}$** | "At 25°C, $K_c = 0.0900$ for $H_2O(g) + Cl_2O(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HOCl(g)$. You start with 1.00 mol $H_2O$, 1.00 mol $Cl_2O$, and 0 mol $HOCl$ in a 1.00 L flask. At equilibrium, what are all concentrations?" | ① $K_c$ expression from balanced equation ② ICE table setup (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) ③ Solving quadratic from ICE table ④ $K_p$ from $K_c$: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$ ⑤ Reaction quotient $Q$: predicting direction ⑥ Le Chatelier's principle: shift predictions with numbers | Ch 18 | 150 min |
| 16 | **Acid-Base pH Calculations** | "Calculate the pH of: (a) 0.025 M $HCl$, (b) 0.025 M $CH_3COOH$ ($K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$), (c) 0.025 M $Ba(OH)_2$. Now calculate the pH of a buffer containing 0.50 M $CH_3COOH$ and 0.50 M $CH_3COONa$." | ① Strong acid: pH = $-\log[H^+]$ ② Strong base: pOH = $-\log[OH^-]$, pH = 14 − pOH ③ Weak acid: $K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}$, ICE table → quadratic or approximation ④ Weak base: $K_b$, same pattern ⑤ $K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 1.0\times 10^{-14}$ ⑥ Buffer: Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH = $pK_a + \log\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]}$ ⑦ Polyprotic acids: stepwise $K_a$ values | Ch 17 | 180 min |
| 17 | **$K_{sp}$, Solubility & Precipitation** | "Will a precipitate form when 50.0 mL of 0.0020 M $AgNO_3$ is mixed with 50.0 mL of 0.00010 M $NaCl$? ($K_{sp}$ of $AgCl = 1.8 \times 10^{-10}$). What is the molar solubility of $Ag_2CrO_4$ in pure water? ($K_{sp} = 1.1\times 10^{-12}$)" | ① $K_{sp}$ expression from formula ② Molar solubility ($s$) from $K_{sp}$ ③ $K_{sp}$ from solubility data ④ Common ion effect on solubility ⑤ $Q_{sp}$ vs. $K_{sp}$: predicting precipitation ⑥ Selective precipitation: which ion drops out first | Ch 18 | 150 min |

| # | Topic | Opening Problem | Calculation Skills Built | Big Fat Notebook | Time |

| 18 | **Oxidation Numbers & Redox Balancing** | "Balance this in acidic solution: $MnO_4^- + Fe^{2+} \to Mn^{2+} + Fe^{3+}$. Now balance this in basic solution: $Cr(OH)_3 + ClO^- \to CrO_4^{2-} + Cl^-$." | ① Assigning oxidation numbers by the rules ② Identifying what is oxidized and what is reduced ③ Half-reaction method (acidic): split → balance atoms (except O, H) → add $H_2O$ for O → add $H^+$ for H → add $e^-$ for charge ④ Half-reaction method (basic): do acidic first, then neutralize $H^+$ with $OH^-$ ⑤ Multiplying half-reactions so electrons cancel | Ch 19 | 150 min |

## The Identity of This Curriculum

```
What a student who "read the book but still can't do the problems" uniquely lacks:
→ The ability to see a word problem and immediately know which calculation pattern applies.
→ The volume of practice needed for exam-speed fluency.

Every session of this curriculum repeats one pattern:
1. A problem that looks impossible opens the session.
2. You work through 5–8 worked examples that share a hidden structure.
3. You extract the procedure — the 3-step template.
4. You grind 10 Basic Drills until the template is automatic.
5. You grind 10 Advanced Drills until you can handle any exam variation.
6. THEN you read the concept chapter to understand WHY the template works.

20 sessions × 20 drills = 400 problems.
That is not overkill. That is exam readiness.

The calculator comes first. The concept seals it in.
The volume makes it permanent.
```
